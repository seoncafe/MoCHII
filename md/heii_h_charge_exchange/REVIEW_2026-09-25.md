## Decision

Do not apply an additional factor of 1/4 to the Kingdon & Ferland (1996;
KF96) term on the evidence currently available. This is a provisional
implementation decision, not a certification of the present rate.

The next task is to establish both the normalization and the temperature
dependence of the nonradiative rate from cross sections with an explicit
initial-state convention. A small effect on a particular nebular model is
not a physical justification for retaining or changing a rate.

| Question | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Does an unpolarized He+(1s) + H(1s) pair have singlet probability 1/4? | Yes, for the electronic spin statistics used here. |
| Does a cross section conditional on singlet incidence need this factor? | Yes, when converting it to the rate for an unpolarized gas. |
| Does a rate already averaged over entrance spins need another factor? | No. |
| Is the published radiative correction explicit? | Yes; Stancil & Zygelman (1996), p. 107, footnote 1. |
| Should MoCHII's radiative fit receive another factor of 1/4? | No. Its normalization is consistent with the corrected radiative rates. |
| Has the normalization of the KF96 He+ fit been established? | No. |
| Is a constant rescaling sufficient to validate the KF96 term as nonradiative? | Not established; the temperature dependence must also be checked. |

## Physical definition

The channels carried in the current implementation are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{He^+ + H} &\longrightarrow \mathrm{He + H^+}+h\nu,
&&\text{radiative},\\
\mathrm{He^+ + H} &\longrightarrow \mathrm{He + H^+},
&&\text{nonradiative}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Each ground-state reactant is an electronic doublet. An unpolarized pair has
singlet probability 1/4 and triplet probability 3/4. In the approximation of
conserved electronic spin, formation of ground-state He(1s2 1S) proceeds
through the singlet entrance channel. Thus

\[
k_{\rm nr}^{\rm unpol}(T)
=\frac14\left\langle\sigma_{\rm nr}^{S=0}(E)v\right\rangle_T.
\]

Spin averaging and Maxwell averaging are separate operations. The factor
must be included exactly once. Its physical necessity does not establish
whether it is missing from a published fit.

Courtney et al. (2021), equations (4)-(6), give the approach probability

\[
p_{\Lambda S}=\frac{(2S+1)(2-\delta_{0\Lambda})}
{(2L_a+1)(2S_a+1)(2L_b+1)(2S_b+1)},
\]

which is 1/4 for this singlet entrance state. Their calculation addresses
radiative association and radiative charge transfer; it does not determine
the normalization of the KF96 nonradiative interpretation.

The triplet entrance channel cannot form ground-state helium within this
spin-conserving approximation. Charge transfer to excited triplet helium
requires sufficient collision energy. Do not describe all triplet product
channels as closed at every thermal temperature. Zygelman et al. (1989)
neglect triplet transfer and discuss its possible importance at higher
energies on p. 2345.

## What the published sources establish

### Radiative channel

Zygelman et al. (1989), Section II.B, treat radiative transfer separately from
the nonradiative calculation of Section II.A. Table I contains radiative
rates through 1000 K; its column labeled "Direct" is radiative charge
transfer, not the nonradiative reaction. Figure 8 compares the two processes.

Stancil & Zygelman (1996), p. 107, footnote 1, explicitly correct the
radiative rates in Zygelman et al. (1989) and the radiative association rates
in Zygelman & Dalgarno (1990) by 1/4. This footnote does not explicitly
correct nonradiative rates. The scan was checked visually because the PDF
has no useful searchable body text.

Stancil, Lepp & Dalgarno (1998), Table 1, reaction (19), give
`1.20e-15 * (T/300)^0.25 cm^3 s^-1`. Reference (10) in the table notes
states that the Zygelman radiative result was multiplied by 0.25 for the
approach probability.

MoCHII uses Glover & Jappsen (2007), Table 1, R26:
`1.25e-15 * (T/300)^0.25 cm^3 s^-1`. This is a fit consistent with the
corrected normalization, not an exact quarter of every tabulated value.
Agreement with the corrected low-temperature data does not validate its
extrapolation to arbitrary temperatures.

### KF96 term and later collision calculations

KF96, equation (7) and Table 1, list the He+ parameters
`a = 7.47e-6`, `b = 2.06`, `c = 9.93`, and `d = -3.89`, where `a` is
in units of `1e-9 cm^3 s^-1`. The fitted interval is 6000-100000 K and the
listed source is Zygelman et al. (1989). Section 3.1 cautions against
extrapolation significantly outside the tabulated intervals.

The passages inspected do not establish whether the He+ fit already
contains the singlet approach probability. KF96 describes the entries as
total rates; that wording alone does not document the radiative and
nonradiative decomposition assumed by MoCHII. Recovering the input data
behind this row would resolve both its normalization and its interpretation.

Kimura et al. (1993) calculate the reverse process, H+ + He -> H + He+.
Their published Table 1 and Table 3 are useful comparison material, but
their low-temperature rate table should not be substituted without a
consistency check against the cross sections and the reaction threshold.

Loreau et al. (2014), Sections 2 and 3.1, provide an independent calculation
of this reverse process. Their two quantum methods agree with one another,
while they report discrepancies with Kimura et al. (1993). Section 3.4 and
Table 2 supply broad energy-range cross-section fits. Use the ground-state
capture process (1) for a comparison with the ground-state reaction here.
Energy units, the physical threshold, and the behavior of the fit near that
threshold require explicit checks before a Maxwell average is accepted.
The paper does not state whether KF96 applied a factor of 1/4.

Loreau et al. (2018) primarily treat excited n = 2, 3 reactants. Those rates
are not a direct replacement for ground-state He+ + H. Del Zanna et al.
(2020), Section 2.7, describe use of Zygelman's ground-state cross sections
with a high-energy extension using Loreau et al. (2014). This supplies a
useful provenance link, not a resolution of the KF96 normalization.

### Status of the exploratory Figure 8 calculation

During the review, a rough numerical check used visual estimates of
Zygelman's Figure 8 points, log interpolation, and assumed values outside
the visible sample. Its difference from KF96 changed with temperature.
This motivates checking the shape of the rate as well as its normalization.

That calculation was not a controlled digitization. The low-energy samples,
interpolation uncertainty, energy conventions, and spin normalization were
not established, and the integration emitted a subdivision warning. Its
numbers are not adopted as reference data and are not used in the formula
comparison stored here. It does not prove that KF96 omitted the factor or
that a particular replacement rate is correct.

## Present implementation and numerical comparison

The code paths are recorded in [CODE_PATHS.md](CODE_PATHS.md). With
`t4 = min(T, 1e5)/1e4`, MoCHII currently evaluates

\[
\begin{aligned}
k_{\rm rad} &=1.25\times10^{-15}(T/300)^{0.25},\\
k_{\rm nr} &=7.47\times10^{-15}t_4^{2.06}
                 [1+9.93\exp(-3.89t_4)],\\
k_{\rm forward} &=k_{\rm rad}+k_{\rm nr}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Rates below are in `cm^3 s^-1`, computed by
[rate_comparison.py](calculations/rate_comparison.py). This is a Python
evaluation of the inspected formulas, not a call into the Fortran module.
The quarter-scaled column is a sensitivity scenario, not a recommendation.

| T [K] | Radiative | KF96 term as used | Current total | Total with 0.25 times the KF96 term | Ratio to current total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5000 | 2.525644e-15 | 4.335005e-15 | 6.860649e-15 | 3.609396e-15 | 0.526101 |
| 6000 | 2.643428e-15 | 5.117706e-15 | 7.761134e-15 | 3.922855e-15 | 0.505449 |
| 10000 | 3.003514e-15 | 8.986576e-15 | 1.199009e-14 | 5.250158e-15 | 0.437875 |
| 20000 | 3.571801e-15 | 3.127817e-14 | 3.484997e-14 | 1.139134e-14 | 0.326868 |
| 100000 | 5.341088e-15 | 8.576708e-13 | 8.630118e-13 | 2.197588e-13 | 0.254642 |

The 5000 K row is below KF96's fitted range. The code extrapolates the
expression below 6000 K and holds its temperature argument at 100000 K
above the upper limit. The source comment that curve crossing requires
the particular low-temperature power `T^2.06` is not justified by the
evidence reviewed. That exponent belongs to this empirical fit; it is not
a general physical law for charge transfer.

For the nonradiative ground-state pair, the existing reverse relation is

\[
k_{\rm reverse}=4\exp[-\Delta E/(k_{\rm B}T)]k_{\rm nr},
\qquad \Delta E\simeq10.989\ {\rm eV},
\]

with the reduced-mass ratio approximated as unity, as in the code. This
factor of 4 is the ratio of atomic statistical weights. It must remain in
the relation if the normalization of `k_nr` is changed. The collisional
reverse applies to the nonradiative channel; reversing the radiative
channel requires a radiation field.

The energy transfer from this nonradiative pair is

\[
\Gamma_{\rm nr}=\Delta E\,[
k_{\rm nr}n_{\rm He^+}n_{\rm H^0}
-k_{\rm reverse}n_{\rm He^0}n_{\rm H^+}],
\]

with the energy converted to erg. A change to the common nonradiative
coefficient must propagate to the forward total, its reverse, and this heat
term together. MoCHII already expresses those dependencies through one
function. This review does not certify the separate He++ reactions in the
same module.

## Cloudy and the benchmark argument

The inspected Cloudy c25.00 implementation contains a table contribution
with the KF96 shape and a rounded amplitude of approximately one quarter,
plus a separate full KF96 contribution. The table contribution clamps
temperature to 6000-100000 K; the added contribution uses the actual
temperature. Therefore the result is approximately `1.25 * KF96(T)` only
inside that interval, before optional overrides. The comments identify the
table contribution as radiative, which its formula does not support.
This implementation cannot establish the correct physical normalization.
See [the code review](CODE_PATHS.md#cloudy-c2500-comparison).

The earlier note quoted changes of 0.011 K and 0.003 K in Lexington
benchmarks with all He-H charge exchange toggled. These are historical
reported values, not measurements made or independently revalidated in
this review. In a coupled nonlinear ionization and thermal calculation,
such a toggle does not supply a rigorous upper bound on the response to
changing one coefficient. No conclusion about physical correctness follows
from those reported effect sizes.

## Work needed before changing the rate

1. Obtain the numerical input behind the KF96 He+ row or cross sections
   whose entrance-spin normalization is explicit. Verify whether radiative
   contributions enter the fitted quantity. If clarification from the
   authors is needed, formulate that specific question; no contact has been
   initiated as part of this work.
2. Compare ground-state cross sections for the two reaction directions
   using microscopic reversibility. Align the energy zero, the 10.989 eV
   defect, the reduced masses, statistical weights, and `eV` versus `eV/u`
   conventions. Assess near-threshold data before using a broad-range fit.
3. Form the Maxwell average from those verified cross sections:
   \[
   k(T)=\sqrt{\frac{8}{\pi\mu}}(k_{\rm B}T)^{-3/2}
   \int_0^\infty E\sigma(E)e^{-E/(k_{\rm B}T)}\,dE.
   \]
   Use consistent units and apply the singlet factor only if the cross
   sections are conditional on singlet incidence. Quantify interpolation
   and missing-energy contributions.
4. Keep the radiative and nonradiative channels explicit, document their
   validity intervals, and derive the nonradiative reverse and heating
   from the same adopted coefficient.
5. Validate the revised coefficient against independent physical data.
   Then run focused H/He balance and heating checks for the affected code
   paths. A test that repeats the fit coefficients alone cannot establish
   the spin normalization. Revisit full nebular models only when required
   by the validated change and the intended application.

## Scope of this organization

The published PDFs were collected from the existing local reference
directory and the research files downloaded during the preceding review.
The original PDF bytes are preserved. The source guide records the
sections actually used; it does not imply a comprehensive validation of
