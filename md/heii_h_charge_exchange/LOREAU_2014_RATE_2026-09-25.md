Date: 2026-09-25.

Script and output:
- `calculations/loreau2014_rate.py`;
- `calculations/loreau2014_rate.txt`.

No code, data or simulation was changed.

## Calculation

**Source.** Loreau, Ryabchenko & Vaeck (2014, J. Phys. B 47, 135204) compute
the reverse reaction, their process (1),

  H⁺ + He(1s² ¹S) → H(1s) + He⁺(1s),

with two fully quantal methods below 1 keV/u, and the two agree. Their fit
(eq. 3, Table 2) gives σ in 10⁻¹⁶ cm² as a Fourier series in log₁₀E, with E
in eV/u, a stated uncertainty < 25%, and quantal data from 15 eV/u. The
log₁₀ reading reproduces their Figure 1 (2.3 × 10⁻⁷ × 10⁻¹⁶ cm² at
15 eV/u).

**Reciprocity.** Both channels are in their ground states.
- Statistical weights: g_f = 2·2 = 4 for He⁺(²S) + H(²S), and g_r = 1 for
  He(¹S) + H⁺.
- Microscopic reversibility then gives the unpolarized forward cross
  section

  σ_f(E_f) = ¼ · (μ_r E_r)/(μ_f E_f) · σ_r(E_r),  E_r = E_f + 10.989 eV (CM).

- The ¼ is the ratio of statistical weights. It enters without any
  assumption about how another fit was normalized.
- The forward rate is the Maxwell average of σ_f v.
- Check: averaging σ_r directly over the reverse channel reproduces
  k_r = 4 (μ_f/μ_r)^{3/2} e^{−ΔE/kT} k_f to 1.000000 at every temperature.

**Energy frame.** Loreau et al. do not state it. E is taken as the lab
energy per nucleon of the proton projectile, the usual convention for
p + He, so that E_cm = E·m_p m_He/(m_p + m_He). The ground-capture threshold
is then 13.66 eV/u, and their data start at a forward energy of 1.08 eV.
Reading E directly as E_cm is evaluated as a sensitivity case.

**Below 15 eV/u** there are no quantal data. Three variants bracket the
rate: the fit as given, σ = 0, and σ held at its 15 eV/u value.

**Uncertainty band.**
- Above ~6000 K, the near-threshold bracket, the energy-frame reading and
  the ±25% of the fit together span about a factor of 2–4. At 10⁴ K that is
  3×10⁻¹⁷ – 1.7×10⁻¹⁶.
- Below ~5000 K the rate depends on the unmeasured cross section just above
  threshold. The σ = 0 variant falls by 10⁵ at 1000 K, so there Loreau fixes
  only an upper value, about 4×10⁻¹⁶.

## What it means

### 1. The non-radiative channel is a small correction to the radiative one

- **How big it is:** at every temperature in the table the Loreau rate is
  2.7–9% of the radiative rate, from 3000 K up. At 1000 K the upper value is
  20%.
- **What that implies:** the He⁺ + H → He + H⁺ total is set by the radiative
  channel, which MoCHII already carries correctly. Within the band above,
  the non-radiative part changes the total by a few percent at nebular
  temperatures.
- **What MoCHII assumes now:** it takes the opposite view. At 10⁴ K the
  non-radiative term is 75% of the total, 3.0 times the radiative rate. At
  2×10⁴ K it is 90% of the total.

- Loreau does not settle the temperature dependence at high T. Above
  ~3×10⁴ K it disagrees with Zygelman et al. (1989), the source of the
  KF96 fit, by up to 10³ in the cross section.
- Loreau's own trend is nearly flat, about 1×10⁻¹⁶ from 6000 K to
  5×10⁴ K. KF96 instead grows as T².

### 3. Where it matters: He⁺ coexisting with H⁰

He⁺ is removed by radiative recombination at α_B(He I) n_e, and by charge
exchange at k n(H⁰). Their ratio is

  R = k n(H⁰) / [α_B(He I) n_e].

At 10⁴ K, with α_B(He I) = 2.76×10⁻¹³:

| | R per unit n(H⁰)/n_e | charge exchange is a 10% effect when |
|---|---|---|
| MoCHII now | 0.043 | n(H⁰)/n_e ≳ 2.3 |
| with Loreau | 0.011 | n(H⁰)/n_e ≳ 9 |

- **H II regions like the Lexington benchmarks.** He⁺ lives inside the H
  front, where n(H⁰)/n_e ≲ 10⁻², so R < 10⁻³ in both cases. The He⁺ + H
  channel is irrelevant there, whichever rate is used. This is consistent
  with the ≤ 0.01 K reported for switching the whole He–H exchange.
- **Where it does matter:** where He⁺ survives into gas that is mostly
  neutral H:
  - the partly ionized zone beyond the H front under a hard spectrum;
  - X-ray-illuminated or shocked gas;
  - PDR boundaries;
  - the upper atmospheres of irradiated planets.

  Wherever n(H⁰)/n_e ~ 3–30, the current rate makes charge exchange a
  12–130% addition to He⁺ loss at 10⁴ K, while Loreau makes it 3–33%.
- **Hot partly neutral gas (a few 10⁴ – 10⁵ K).**
  - The current KF96 term alone reaches 8.6×10⁻¹³ at 10⁵ K. Against that
    temperature's α_B(He I) (5×10⁻¹³, including the He I dielectronic peak),
    charge exchange would dominate He⁺ loss wherever n(H⁰) ≳ n_e.
  - Loreau puts the same process at 2.4×10⁻¹⁶, negligible.
  - The two calculations are the ones that disagree in this range, so the
    size of this effect is itself uncertain by 10³.

### 4. The reverse reaction and the heating are negligible either way

- **Reverse reaction.** He + H⁺ → He⁺ + H is 4 e^{−ΔE/kT} k_f. At 10⁴ K
  that is 1.5×10⁻²¹ (Loreau), against 1.0×10⁻¹⁹ now. As a He⁰ ionization
  rate it is ~10⁻¹⁹ s⁻¹ at n_p = 100 cm⁻³, far below any photoionization
  rate.
- **Heating.** The non-radiative heating (10.989 eV per event) falls with
  k_f by the same factor, 71× at 10⁴ K. It was already 3×10⁻⁶ of the
  photoheating on the converged HII40 state (charge-exchange heating
  measurement, 2026-09-25).

### 5. The same fit sits in EXHALE

EXHALE uses the same pair:
- the radiative channel B2 as 1.25×10⁻¹⁵ (T/300)^0.25;
- the KF96 non-radiative term, `a = 7.47d-6, b = 2.06, c = 9.93, d = −3.89`
  (`src/modules/radiation/charge_exchange.f90:2015–2016`).

In an irradiated planetary atmosphere, He⁺ recombines in gas where
n(H⁰)/n_e ≫ 1 in the lower thermosphere. The He⁺ → He branch into 2³S is
what the 10830 Å absorption is built on. There the current rate overstates
He⁺ removal by charge exchange 3.8× at 10⁴ K, and more at higher
temperature. The size of the consequence for the metastable population
depends on how much of He⁺'s loss charge exchange carries in a given model;
this note does not estimate it. EXHALE was not modified.

## Limits of this calculation

- **Energy frame:** the eV/u convention is inferred, not stated by the
  authors. The CM reading lowers every value by 25–35%.
- **Near threshold:** the region is not covered by the quantal data. Below
  ~5000 K the result is an upper value only.
- **Validity of the fit:** the ±25% of the fit is the authors' statement
  for their data range. Its behavior between the threshold and 15 eV/u is
  not constrained.
- **Neglected corrections:** the reduced masses of the two channels differ
  by electron masses only, and the fine-structure-free ¹S/²S weights are
  exact for these ground states.
- **The high-temperature conflict:** above ~3×10⁴ K, Loreau (2014) and
  Zygelman et al. (1989) disagree. Loreau's two methods agree with each
  other and with the Latypov & Shaporenko data, and the authors report
  disagreement with the Zygelman group's reverse calculation (Kimura
  et al. 1993). This note records the conflict, not a resolution.
