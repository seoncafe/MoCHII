# He⁺ + H Non-Radiative Charge Exchange: Quantitative Analysis (2026-09-25)

> **Record of 2026-09-25.** Statements about the code ("current", "MoCHII uses",
> "as used", EXHALE's rates) are as of 2026-09-25.  Since 2026-09-26 both codes use
> the Loreau et al. (2014) non-radiative rate by reciprocity (9.64e-17 cm^3 s^-1 at
> 1e4 K) and the Stancil, Lepp & Dalgarno (1998) radiative rate
> 1.20e-15 (T/300)^0.25; see `README.md`.

This note reads `REVIEW_2026-09-25.md` and pursues its open items with two
calculations the review did not make:

1. a Maxwell average of the Zygelman et al. (1989) Figure 8 points, to test
   the normalization of the KF96 fit against its own source;
2. the forward rate implied by the independent reverse-direction calculation
   of Loreau, Ryabchenko & Vaeck (2014), obtained through microscopic
   reversibility. In that route the spin statistics enter by construction,
   and nothing has to be assumed about a missing factor.

Scripts and their recorded output are in `calculations/`:
- `zygelman1989_fig8_rate.py`, `.txt`;
- `loreau2014_reciprocity_rate.py`, `.txt`.

## Findings

1. **At 5×10⁴–10⁵ K the KF96 He⁺ fit lacks the 1/4.** There it reproduces
   Zygelman's non-radiative cross sections to 9–17%, with no approach factor
   applied. Those cross sections are conditional on singlet approach:
   Zygelman et al. (1989), p. 2340, write "it sufficed to include only
   molecular states which correspond to the initial A ¹Σ⁺ and final X ¹Σ⁺
   channels". An unpolarized gas therefore needs a further ×1/4 there.
2. **At H II-region temperatures the KF96 fit is not what its own source
   gives.** Zygelman's Figure 8 bounds the singlet-conditional non-radiative
   rate at 10⁴ K to ≤ 8.2×10⁻¹⁶ cm³ s⁻¹. That makes the unpolarized rate
   ≤ 2.1×10⁻¹⁶. KF96 gives 9.0×10⁻¹⁵, which is **≥ 11×** the
   singlet-conditional bound and **≥ 44×** the unpolarized one. The 1/4
   question is therefore secondary at nebular temperatures.
3. **Loreau (2014), taken through reciprocity, gives an unpolarized forward
   rate of 1.1–2.4×10⁻¹⁶ cm³ s⁻¹ at 6×10³–10⁵ K.** At 10⁴ K this agrees with
   Zygelman's Figure 8 average ×1/4 (9.3×10⁻¹⁷). At high energy the two
   calculations disagree by up to ~10³, and the gap opens where Zygelman's
   cross section rises steeply above 4 eV.
4. **In MoCHII the non-radiative term is overstated by ~40–70× at 10⁴ K.**
   Both independent readings put the physical non-radiative channel at
   ~1×10⁻¹⁶ cm³ s⁻¹ there, while the code uses 9×10⁻¹⁵. That is below the
   radiative channel (3.0×10⁻¹⁵), not three times above it. The He + H⁺
   reverse rate and the 10.989 eV heating term inherit the same factor.

## 1. The KF96 fit against its own source

**Reading the figure.** Zygelman et al. (1989) Figure 8 plots the
non-radiative (circles) and radiative (triangles) cross sections in units
of a₀² against collision energy from 1 to 100 eV. The page was rendered at
400 dpi and the eight circles were read on the frame:
- x axis: 1 eV and 100 eV at the frame edges;
- y axis: ticks at 10⁻², 10⁻³, 10⁻⁴ a₀².

The radiative triangles read 3.6×10⁻⁴ a₀² = 1.0×10⁻²⁰ cm². This matches the
text ("an almost constant value of about 10⁻²⁰ cm²"), which confirms the
scale.

| E [eV] | σ_nr [a₀²] | σ_nr [cm²] |
|---|---|---|
| 4.82 | 4.6×10⁻⁵ | 1.3×10⁻²¹ |
| 7.06 | 8.0×10⁻⁴ | 2.2×10⁻²⁰ |
| 8.79 | 1.7×10⁻³ | 4.9×10⁻²⁰ |
| 9.44 | 2.2×10⁻³ | 6.1×10⁻²⁰ |
| 11.75 | 3.5×10⁻³ | 9.7×10⁻²⁰ |
| 16.97 | 6.6×10⁻³ | 1.9×10⁻¹⁹ |
| 25.15 | 1.2×10⁻² | 3.3×10⁻¹⁹ |
| 39.76 | 2.1×10⁻² | 5.9×10⁻¹⁹ |

The reading precision is a few percent in log σ per point. The text states
that the non-radiative cross section "show[s] a sharp increase above 4 eV";
no circle is plotted below 4.8 eV within the 10⁻⁵ a₀² floor of the axis.

**Maxwell average.** The points were joined log-log. Below 4.8 eV the
steepness of the first segment (slope 7.5) was continued, and above 40 eV
the slope of the last two segments (1.35) was continued. The reduced mass
is that of He⁺ + H.

| T [K] | Fig. 8, singlet | Fig. 8 × 1/4 | KF96 | KF96 / Fig. 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 000 | 9.8×10⁻¹⁸ | 2.4×10⁻¹⁸ | 5.1×10⁻¹⁵ | 525 |
| 10 000 | 3.7×10⁻¹⁶ | 9.3×10⁻¹⁷ | 9.0×10⁻¹⁵ | 24 |
| 20 000 | 1.3×10⁻¹⁴ | 3.2×10⁻¹⁵ | 3.1×10⁻¹⁴ | 2.4 |
| 50 000 | 2.3×10⁻¹³ | 5.7×10⁻¹⁴ | 2.1×10⁻¹³ | 0.91 |
| 100 000 | 1.0×10⁻¹² | 2.6×10⁻¹³ | 8.6×10⁻¹³ | 0.83 |

**Where the tail cannot be the issue.** At 5×10⁴ and 10⁵ K the Maxwellian
weight lies mostly over the plotted 5–40 eV, so the extrapolations matter
little there. At those temperatures KF96 equals Zygelman's
singlet-conditional rate to 9–17%, and it is 3.3–3.6× the value that
carries the approach factor. A reading error of a few percent cannot turn
0.83–0.91 into 0.25. **This is where the KF96 fit is established as
omitting the 1/4.**

**At 10⁴ K the bound does not depend on the extrapolation.**
- Every circle below 4.8 eV lies below the axis floor, σ < 10⁻⁵ a₀²
  (2.8×10⁻²² cm²).
- That part therefore contributes less than σ v̄ = 4.5×10⁻¹⁶.
- The plotted part (≥ 4.8 eV) contributes 3.7×10⁻¹⁶.
- The singlet-conditional non-radiative rate is therefore
  ≤ 8.2×10⁻¹⁶ cm³ s⁻¹, and the unpolarized rate ≤ 2.1×10⁻¹⁶.

KF96's 9.0×10⁻¹⁵ exceeds its own source's bound by ≥ 11× even before the
approach factor. Below ~2×10⁴ K the fitted shape
[1 + 9.93 exp(−3.89 t₄)] carries a low-temperature excess that nothing in
Figure 8 supports. What it was fitted to below 2×10⁴ K is not documented in
KF96. Its magnitude at 6000 K (5.1×10⁻¹⁵) is of the order of Zygelman's
singlet-conditional *radiative* rate, but that is an observation, not an
identification. If the radiative channel is what the low-temperature part
contains, then MoCHII, which adds the radiative channel separately, counts
it twice there.

## 2. An independent determination: Loreau et al. (2014) via reciprocity

**The reverse calculation.** Loreau, Ryabchenko & Vaeck (2014) compute the
reverse reaction, process (1): H⁺ + He(1s²) → H(1s) + He⁺(1s). They used
two fully quantal methods below 1 keV/u, which agree with each other, and
their results agree with the low-energy measurements of Latypov &
Shaporenko. They also report a disagreement with Kimura et al. (1993), which
used the Zygelman group's potential curves. Table 2 gives a Fourier fit in
log₁₀E:
- E is in eV/u;
- σ is in 10⁻¹⁶ cm²;
- the stated uncertainty is < 25% from 10 eV/u to 10 MeV/u;
- the log₁₀ reading reproduces their Figure 1 (2.3×10⁻⁷ at 15 eV/u).

**From reverse to forward.**
- The entrance channel He(¹S) + H⁺ is a singlet.
- By microscopic reversibility the unpolarized forward cross section is

  σ_f(E_f) = (g_r/g_f)·(μ_r E_r)/(μ_f E_f)·σ_r(E_r),
  with g_r/g_f = 1/4 and E_r = E_f + 10.989 eV (CM).

  Here g_f = 2·2 = 4 counts the spin states of He⁺(²S) + H(²S), and
  g_r = 1 those of He(¹S) + H⁺. The 1/4 appears here from the statistical
  weights, not as an assumption about any fit.
- **Energy convention.** E is converted as lab energy per nucleon of the
  proton projectile, E_cm = (E/u)·m_p m_He/(m_p + m_He). The threshold is
  then 13.66 eV/u. Their quantal data start at 15 eV/u, which is a forward
  energy of 1.08 eV.
- **Below 15 eV/u**, i.e. at forward energies under 1.08 eV, three variants
  bound the rate: the fit as given, σ set to zero, and σ held at its
  15 eV/u value.

| T [K] | k_f from Loreau (fit) [zero, flat below 15 eV/u] | KF96 | KF96 / Loreau |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 000 | 1.5×10⁻¹⁶ [2.3×10⁻¹⁷, 1.6×10⁻¹⁶] | 5.1×10⁻¹⁵ | 34 |
| 10 000 | 1.3×10⁻¹⁶ [4.4×10⁻¹⁷, 1.3×10⁻¹⁶] | 9.0×10⁻¹⁵ | 71 |
| 20 000 | 1.1×10⁻¹⁶ [6.9×10⁻¹⁷, 1.1×10⁻¹⁶] | 3.1×10⁻¹⁴ | 290 |
| 50 000 | 1.2×10⁻¹⁶ [1.1×10⁻¹⁶, 1.2×10⁻¹⁶] | 2.1×10⁻¹³ | 1 700 |
| 100 000 | 2.4×10⁻¹⁶ [2.4×10⁻¹⁶, 2.4×10⁻¹⁶] | 8.6×10⁻¹³ | 3 500 |

**Agreement and disagreement with Zygelman.**
- *At 10⁴ K they agree.* Loreau gives 1.3×10⁻¹⁶ and Zygelman ×1/4 gives
  9.3×10⁻¹⁷, within 40%. Both fall inside Zygelman's own bound of
  ≤ 2.1×10⁻¹⁶.
- *Above ~5 eV they do not.* At a forward energy of 40 eV, Zygelman's
  σ = 5.9×10⁻¹⁹ cm² corresponds to σ_r ≈ 4.6×10⁻¹⁹ at 63 eV/u. Loreau's fit
  gives ~3×10⁻²² there and reaches 4.6×10⁻¹⁹ only near 2 keV/u, so the two
  differ by ~10³.
- *How the conflict stands:*
  - Loreau's two methods agree with each other.
  - Loreau agrees with the Latypov & Shaporenko measurement.
  - Loreau states the disagreement with Kimura et al. (1993), which used the
    same potential curves as Zygelman.

  Settling that conflict is not needed for H II-region temperatures, where
  the two agree.

## 3. Assessment of the review's open questions

| Review question | Status after this analysis |
|---|---|
| Has the normalization of the KF96 He⁺ fit been established? | At 5×10⁴–10⁵ K, yes: it is Zygelman's singlet-conditional rate, without the 1/4 (KF96/Fig. 8 = 0.83–0.91). |
| Is a constant rescaling sufficient? | No. Below 2×10⁴ K the fit exceeds its own source's bound by ≥ 11× (≥ 44× unpolarized), so no constant factor makes it right. |
| Does an independent calculation settle the nebular rate? | Yes, for 6×10³–2×10⁴ K. Loreau via reciprocity and Zygelman ×1/4 agree at 1×10⁻¹⁶ (10⁴ K), within the stated bounds. Above ~3×10⁴ K the two quantum calculations disagree by up to 10³. |
| Is the radiative channel in MoCHII correct? | Yes (1.25×10⁻¹⁵ (T/300)^0.25, with the 1/4). At nebular temperatures it is the larger channel. |

## 4. Consequence for MoCHII (not applied)

- **The rate.** `nonradiative_HeII_H` gives 9.0×10⁻¹⁵ at 10⁴ K, against
  ~1×10⁻¹⁶ from both physical readings.
  - The forward He⁺ + H total becomes 3.1×10⁻¹⁵ instead of 1.2×10⁻¹⁴
    (0.26×).
  - He + H⁺ and the non-radiative heating fall ~70× with it.
- **Direction of the correction.** It lowers He⁺ destruction by H⁰ and
  hydrogen's charge-exchange ionization, in partly neutral gas.
- **A proposed replacement,** for the author to decide:
  - tabulate the unpolarized forward rate from Loreau (2014) by
    reciprocity, offline, with its near-threshold bracket, over
    3×10³–3×10⁴ K where the two calculations agree;
  - state the validity range in the code;
  - above ~3×10⁴ K the value is uncertain by up to 10³ between the two
    calculations, and the documentation must carry that rather than pick
    silently.

  The reverse rate and the heating then follow from the one function, as
  now.
- **The comment to fix with it.** In `ion_balance_mod.f90` the claim that
  "it falls as t4^2.06, as a curve-crossing channel must" should go, as the
  review noted. The t4^2.06 is a property of the fit, and below 2×10⁴ K the
  fit is not what its source says.

## Scope and limits

- Figure 8 was digitized by eye from a 400-dpi rendering. Only the ratios at
  5×10⁴–10⁵ K and the bound at 10⁴ K are used as conclusions, and both are
  robust to a few-percent reading error.
- The eV/u convention of Loreau (2014) was taken as projectile lab energy
  per nucleon. The quantal data start above threshold, and the
  near-threshold variants bracket the part that is extrapolated.
- Not read for this note:
  - Kimura et al. (1993) in detail, beyond the earlier MoCHII reading of its
    p. 802;
  - Latypov & Shaporenko (1976);
  - the calculation behind KF96's low-temperature form, which KF96 does not
    document.
- No Fortran was changed and no simulation was run.
