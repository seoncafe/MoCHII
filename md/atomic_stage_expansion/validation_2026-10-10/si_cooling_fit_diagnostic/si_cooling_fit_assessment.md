# S I low-density cooling fit assessment

The corrected S I source admits a 21-term signed exponential fit in the existing cooling-table format. The fit represents the total optically thin, low-density cooling function over **1,000–100,000 K**. Its coefficients are mathematical basis weights, not individual transition rates. The code does not establish physical accuracy outside that interval.

`tools/fitting/fit_cooling.py` now tries a constrained linear minimax expansion only when its existing positive and short signed fits fail. The common excitation temperature comes from `ground_excitation_ladder`; each candidate adds uniform temperature offsets. The linear program limits relative error and cancellation over 801 source temperatures, with additional sampled positivity constraints. Candidate term counts are 18, 24, 32, 40, 48, 56, and 64. The written coefficients are rounded to the existing 16-digit table precision. The code then checks the rounded fit on 801 and 1,601 temperatures in the source interval, and checks nonnegative values on 10,001 sampled temperatures from 1 to 1e9 K. Exact zero coefficients are omitted from the output, leaving 21 stored terms for S I. This is a representation change within the current table format; source collision strengths and the physical cooling calculation are unchanged.

The normal `F.generate` path produced [`normal_generator/cooling_s_1.txt`](normal_generator/cooling_s_1.txt), SHA-256 `6fc94014cd6c6efaf16c450a1d0d35c5b0bcfc5226f980f85892b973ed492040`. Independent reading of that rounded file gave a maximum relative source error of **0.4969645204%** on both the 801- and 1,601-point logarithmic grids, at 8,963.961859 K. The maximum cancellation ratio on the 801-point grid was **54,185.879**, below the specified `1e5` limit. The 10,001-point extended scan was nonnegative; its minimum was `1.2336968677e-268` and maximum `1.7006685528e-19` erg cm^3 s^-1. These sampled values do not prove positivity or accuracy at every temperature outside the fit interval.

The linked Fortran cooling reader returned a maximum relative source error of **0.4969645205%** on 801 points. Its extended scan read all 21 terms and found zero negative values. I visually inspected the [21-term full cooling and residual profiles](normal_generator/si_cooling_profile.pdf): the cooling follows the source smoothly, and the residual stays within approximately ±0.5% over 1,000–100,000 K. Exact numerical checks and the native output are in [`normal_generator/validation.json`](normal_generator/validation.json), [`normal_generator/native_801.txt`](normal_generator/native_801.txt), and [`normal_generator/native_extended.txt`](normal_generator/native_extended.txt).

The native S I atom remains unaccepted for density-dependent use. Its collision fits cannot reproduce three exact zero-strength intervals in the original scaled-source table. The 3→4 and 4→5 collision strengths are zero on 320 of 1,601 sampled fit temperatures below approximately 2,512 K, and 3→5 is zero on 560 points below approximately 5,012 K. The 4→5 fitted row reports a 6,189,587% worst error; 3→4 and 3→5 each report 100%. The source nodes and thresholds are recorded in [`si_native_collision_zeros.json`](si_native_collision_zeros.json). A finite smooth Chebyshev expansion cannot preserve these exact zero intervals together with the rising source strengths. The low-density cooling fit should not be installed as a complete S I density-dependent correction until the native collision representation can preserve the source nodes and zero intervals. No production product was installed here.
