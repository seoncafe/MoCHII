# Si I bound-state radiative identity audit

## Finding

The CHIANTI Si I bound model has one positive radiative row whose listed lower-to-upper indices run against the selected physical level energies: `15 14` in `si_1.wgfa`. The level identities, observed wavelength, oscillator strength, and spontaneous rate do not jointly define a consistent transition. The current writer correctly rejects this row. No independently verified replacement rate for this exact transition was found in the checked Stout files or published source material. Thus a finite-density, line-resolved 35-state atom cannot yet be generated by silently reversing the row or changing its rate. The total ground-state coronal cooling remains mathematically defined, as checked below.

## Direct source and implementation checks

The local CHIANTI source files have 36 states, 242 radiative rows, and 630 collision rows. Bound filtering leaves 35 states, 229 radiative rows, and 595 collision rows. Among positive bound radiative rows, only `15 14` reverses selected energy order. The bound collision set has a `14 15` row (`2.154e-3` Ry, Burgess-Tully type 1), so collision identity does not resolve the radiative coefficient. The Stout Si I collision file covers only the ground-triplet levels 1–3; it supplies no independent collision or spontaneous rate for this pair.

| Source state | Configuration and term | Parity; J; g | Observed energy (cm^-1) | Theoretical energy (cm^-1) | Stout energy (cm^-1) |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| CHIANTI 14 / Stout 14 | `3s2 3p 4p 1P1` | even; 1; 3 | 47284.062 | 47294.047 | 47284.061 |
| CHIANTI 15 / Stout 15 | `3s2 3p 3d 1D2` | odd; 2; 5 | 47351.555 | 47530.402 | 47351.554 |

The `wgfa` row is `15 14 1481635.13 3.558e-02 1.328e+02` (wavelength in Angstrom, weighted oscillator strength, and A in s^-1). The observed gap is 67.493 cm^-1, with physical emission from state 15 to 14. Its reciprocal wavelength is 1,481,635.1325 Angstrom, matching the listed wavelength. Opposite parity and `Delta J = 1` permit an electric dipole transition. However, the standard electric dipole identity `A = 6.6703e15 gf/(g_emitter lambda^2)` gives 21.6221 s^-1 with physical emitting weight 5, while the source lists 132.8 s^-1, a factor of 6.14185. The theoretical level gap is 236.355 cm^-1; the rate and oscillator strength imply about 167.266 cm^-1 if the emitting weight is 5. Neither source energy gap matches that inferred frequency. The calculation diagnoses inconsistency; 21.6221 s^-1 is **not** an independently established physical rate.

Stout `si_1.nrg` agrees with the two observed identities and energies to 0.001 cm^-1, but `si_1.tp` has no transition between these states in either direction. Its absence does not validate either CHIANTI A value or a replacement. The local CHIANTI `.wgfa` header credits several calculations and a 2024 `wgfa_tidy` step; it does not identify the provenance or frequency convention of this individual rate.

The invoked `bound_excitation_atom('si', 1)` returns the original row. `write_excitation_atom._energy_pair` requires increasing energy for a radiative pair, and a direct attempted write raised `ValueError: Radiative transition 15->14 reverses physical energy order; identify its multipole and transform A before writing`. Thus this is an established source identity problem that the current writer detects, rather than evidence that a correct Si I cooling table exists. Reversing the row while retaining 132.8 s^-1 would not resolve the oscillator-strength inconsistency.

## Ground-state coronal cooling is distinct from line emission

In the actual 35-state bound model, all 35 states can reach state 1 through directed positive-A decay. State 1 is the sole state without an outgoing A. Removing only the inconsistent `15 14` row leaves those results unchanged. In an energy-consistent completion of this ground-closed decay network, a ground-state excitation loses its full selected excitation energy in photons independently of intermediate branching. The misoriented source row itself does not describe a positive-energy spontaneous photon; graph reachability does not validate its line emission. The actual coronal routine computes the physical ground-excitation energy sum without using that invalid intermediate photon gap. `radiative_cascade_probabilities` deliberately returns unit ground absorption probabilities in this one-closed-state case. `cooling_low_density_limit` is therefore exactly the direct sum of ground-state excitation coefficients times physical selected gaps. Direct evaluation and the invoked routine agreed to floating-point equality at 1,000, 10,000, and 100,000 K: `1.1560440931429987e-21`, `1.3279116066155156e-20`, and `5.912603133558387e-19` erg cm^3 s^-1. This is a mathematical statement about the model's total coronal cooling, not a claim of collision-source accuracy or of valid finite-density populations, individual photon energies, or line emissivities. Those latter quantities still require a coherent spontaneous-transition record.

## Published methods and remaining source requirement

The final [Dufresne, Del Zanna, and Storey (2021), MNRAS 505, 3968–3981](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/505/3/3968/6283727), DOI `10.1093/mnras/stab1498`, describes the Si I model in Section 2.1.3: NIST energies, Froese Fischer radiative calculations with additional ATSP2K rates, and Autostructure distorted-wave electron collisions. The article treats the neutral collision model as unsuitable for accurate line diagnostics while using it for metastable populations and ionization. This supports only an explicitly approximate collision application over a tested numerical domain, not repair of an inconsistent radiative row. The final publisher HTML methods were checked; a final publisher PDF was not available locally. The final [Froese Fischer (2005), Physical Review A 71, 042506](https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.71.042506) individual transition table was not obtained, and its abstract cannot establish the value or convention of this pair. A text search of the final [NIST silicon transition-probability compilation](https://www.nist.gov/document/jpcrd3720081501ppdf) found related state energies but no independently matched probability for this exact pair in the portions checked; that search does not prove the compilation omits it.

A selective correction requires a state-resolved final calculation or primary database record for the physical `3s2 3p 3d 1D2 -> 3s2 3p 4p 1P1` transition, including its A, level identity, and frequency convention. Alternatively, a documented transition line strength plus the frequency used for the published A would permit a controlled rescaling to the selected energy. Until one is verified, neither the 132.8 s^-1 source A nor an A calculated from the rounded `gf` is certified for the selected transition. The 1,000–100,000 K range is a possible numerical adoption domain for a completed source, not a published accuracy range.
