# O IV and O V production requirements

This is a read-only audit of the staged five-stage oxygen element file. It does not activate the staged rates or atoms. The current installed `data/atomic/element_o.txt` has three stages; the staged file has five. The first two transition blocks have the same numerical records in both files. A stand-alone oxygen extension also does not remove the terminal photon-band and collisional-ionization limits of the other installed elements.

## Stage boundaries and source records

I imported the current `make_element_data.py` parsers without writing output and compared their values with the staged rows. The physical thresholds come from Cloudy c25's identified NIST ASD 2022-11-15 ionization-potential snapshot. The fit parameters are separately sourced; a physical threshold does not change a Voronov fit threshold or inner-shell fit edge.

| Transition | Physical threshold, eV | Voronov CI threshold, eV | PHINNER, eV | Occupied PH1 shell thresholds, eV | CX recombination fit interval, K | CX mean gas heat, eV |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| O III to O IV (3) | 54.935538562775 | 54.9 | 584.0 | 584.0, 65.51, 54.935538562775 | 1,000–50,000 | 5.00 |
| O IV to O V (4) | 77.413501786118 | 77.4 | 614.4 | 614.4, 87.37, 77.413501786118 | 1,000–30,000 | 8.47 |

The next O V to O VI threshold is 113.898951780048 eV. Thus the two added outer-shell edges are inside a 100 eV transported photon band, while the next outer-shell edge is above it. `species_terminal_photoionization_check` tests the terminal threshold, but this check does not bound collisional ionization or nonadjacent inner-shell products. Below PHINNER the reader evaluates the VFKY96 `PHOTO` fit. Its `PHSHELL` sum is used at and above PHINNER; the lower PH1 edges in the table do not by themselves imply that the code sums those subshell fits at 100 eV. A separate Auger audit is required before claiming complete multiple-electron ionization from inner-shell absorption.

For transition 3 the source parser returns PH2 `(0.1723, 675.3, 385.2, 6.822, 0.1191, 0.003839, 0.4569)`, CI `(54.9, 1, 9.31e-9, 0.27, 0.27)`, RR `(2.501e-9, 0.7844, 0.5235, 4.47e6, 0.0447, 1.642e5)` and five DR terms. For transition 4 it returns PH2 `(0.2044, 0.8659, 493.1, 8.785, 3.143, 332.8, 42.85)`, CI `(77.4, 0, 1.02e-8, 0.614, 0.27)`, RR `(3.955e-9, 0.7813, 0.6821, 5.076e6, 0, 0)` and seven DR terms. The staged records match those returned parser values at serialization precision. Both RR and DR are the ground-level `M=1` records for the recombining ion, O IV or O V, rather than the preceding ion.

Both new `CXI` rows are zero because the staged source carries only the O IV + H to O III + H+ and O V + H to O IV + H+ directions. These are explicitly source-limited reactions, not a proof that the reverse rate vanishes physically. Their tabulated `CXR` rows have 206 and 326 samples, respectively, and clamp to the published KF96 rate interval endpoints. Direct evaluation of the source fits at 10,000 K is approximately 4.14226e-9 and 2.60181e-10 cm3 s-1. `CXHEAT` uses the separate KF99 Table 1 mean thermal energies, not the full ionization-potential defects. The 5.00 and 8.47 eV means assume ground reactants and low-density radiative relaxation; neither source resolves the product-state populations, line reabsorption, or high-density collisional return of the product excitation. The final KF99 methods explicitly use total rates and mean defects when multiple channels occur. The rate intervals end below the 100,000 K cooling grid, so endpoint holding is a numerical continuation, not published rate validity. KF96's O V + H row is identified as uncertain in the published-rate audit; do not infer an uncertainty percentage from the table fit alone. The final KF96 article was not re-read in this audit, so its methods claims here are limited to the existing checked transcription and current implementation; the final KF99 publisher PDF was read directly.

At the consuming path, `species_ne_prepare` uses the CI, RR, DR, and charge-transfer rows for adjacent-stage fractions; `species_gamma_compute` and `metal_heating` use the photoelectric rates and energy deposition; `metal_ionization_cooling` uses the physical `ETH` for CI thermal loss and the current RR fit for recombination thermal loss; `metal_charge_exchange_heating` uses the carried CX rates and `CXHEAT`. The staged file provides all these rows, but a row's existence and a parser match are not physical validation of a coupled production calculation. The recombination cooling formula uses the temperature derivative of the adopted total RR+DR curve and needs its own finite-value and source-domain check after activation.

## Excitation atoms and remaining physical checks

The current `bound_excitation_atom('o', 4)` returns 147 levels, 2,319 radiative pairs, and 10,731 collision pairs. The O V call returns 166, 2,995, and 13,695. The O IV 35-level staged atom has 203 radiative rows and 595 collision rows. Its earlier full-bound comparison found a maximum 0.43245% cooling difference at the four tested densities, above the stated 0.3% source-cutoff target, even though the native-vs-selected-atom implementation comparison passed. The full 147-level radiation and collision source is therefore the relevant next candidate for density convergence, followed by source-consistent full-population and registry tests. The 29-level O V atom has 133 radiative and 406 collision rows; its earlier 166-level comparison reported at most 0.15124% over three densities through 1e6 cm-3. That result does not certify 1e10 cm-3 or source atomic accuracy. O V's selected energy vector corrects five mixed observed/theoretical radiative inversions using identified Stout target levels. The published scattering resonance positions were not recalculated with the observed-energy splice. Its source record has two radiatively closed states, so the low-density scalar cooling and finite-density population reference must preserve the source's closed-state treatment.

The original O IV and O V electron-excitation source files are numerical CHIANTI data. The staged atom checks demonstrate interpolation and source truncation behavior, not absolute collision-strength or A-value accuracy. Neither ion's staged atom provides photoexcitation, radiation trapping, excited-state ionization, or resolved recombination cascades. Source-specific temperature accuracy, especially close to 1,000 K, is not established by a fitted 1,000–100,000 K numerical interval. A coupled production decision needs the stage fractions, photoheating, CI and recombination cooling, CX heat, and both ions' full cooling and population profiles under identical radiation and thermal inputs. Inspect the complete profiles and source-domain markers; do not use a small fit residual as physical acceptance.

Before installation: (1) review the original final KF96 rate methods and uncertainty note for O IV/O V capture, and identify the usable thermal range for every activated source; (2) validate the complete 147-level O IV atom or a converged physically identified subset at the intended density range, plus O V through the highest intended density; (3) test the actual staged five-stage reader, thermal consumers, and complete coupled profiles with the common photon band and all other elements' terminal limits; (4) record the unavoidable approximations in the active source and retain the original external atomic data. No source file, production atom, coefficient, or application parameter was changed by this audit.
