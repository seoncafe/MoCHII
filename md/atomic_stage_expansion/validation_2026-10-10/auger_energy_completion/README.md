# Auger cascade energy completion within the 64-stage roster

**October 11 correction.** This is a historical 64-stage diagnostic; the
current objective is 77 metal stages. Its blanket requirement for a joint
charge/energy distribution before any thermal implementation was too strong.
The [published mean-energy review](../../validation_2026-10-11/auger_mean_energy/README.md)
establishes that an unconditional mean suffices for the currently implemented
linear full thermalization of metal electrons. Charge onsets and a consistent
energy convention remain unresolved; the source Eq. (7) correction gives a
negative Ca II M1 mean. The historical JSON and figures are preserved. The
state-resolved representation below is a possible complete model, not the
only representation sufficient for mean heating. Nonlinear metal secondary
deposition is future physics; current H/He secondary deposition does not
make that model a prerequisite for the existing metal heat consumer.

**Physical judgment.** The nine reachable two-electron channels of Mg, Fe,
and Ca fit the approved 64 stages, but the inspected local tables do not
provide a joint distribution of final charge, individual electron energies,
fluorescent photons, and residual ionic excitation. Thus the source supports
a charge-channel diagnostic, not an energy-conserving thermal implementation.
No extra ion stages, production data, public input format, or executable
physics were changed in this investigation. The diagnostic represented 64
stages and 53 adjacent transitions; it does not set the current target.

The read-only [source inventory](source_inventory.json), generated by
[inspect_local_sources.py](inspect_local_sources.py), records SHA-256 values
for every inspected source and the nine shell mappings from the earlier
[CDS-checked audit](../auger_audit_cds_checked/auger_channels.json). Its
counts and thresholds were re-read from that JSON and the actual local files;
they were not inferred from a plan.

The independent [charge and energy-bound calculation](charge_energy_bounds.json)
and [script](charge_energy_bounds.py) read the original fixed-column CDS
`table2`, the existing PH1 shell audit, and the currently selected NIST
ionization potentials. For each of the nine shell entries, they record the
physical PH1 shell index, KM subshell indices, source and +1/+2 destination
indices in the 64-stage roster, raw one-/two-electron probabilities, and
event-energy bounds at the PH1 edge, KM edges, final-charge ground threshold,
and 100 eV. Between these boundary points the upper bound is the exact affine
function `E - sum(IP)`; no interpolation of a thermal quantity is involved.
All nine mapped CDS rows have only one-/two-electron outcomes and normalized
probabilities at their printed precision. For the combined PH1 `2p` and `3p`
shells, the mapped KM fine-subshell *charge counts* are identical, so these
marginal probabilities do not require an unprovided subshell mixing weight.
Their energy distributions still do.

## Existing source coverage

The final journal scan of [Kaastra and Mewe (1993), *A&AS* **97**, 443–482](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1993A%26AS...97..443K/abstract)
is at `/nfs/mocafe/kiseon/MoCHII/references/Kaastra_Mewe_1993_AAS_97_443.pdf`.
Its pages 446–447 were inspected as page images in this audit. Section 2.2
says the authors computed a branching tree over vacancy configurations and
removed Auger transitions with nonpositive electron energy. Section 3 says
they accumulated electron-count probabilities and *average* emitted electron
and photon energies from the branches; it also notes that unmodeled final
excitation contributes to the energy discrepancy. The deposited CDS
`table2` gives electron-count marginals and mean Auger energy by initial
vacancy, while `table3` gives mean line yields and energies. The deposited
tables do not preserve each branch's endpoint and energy ledger. The
published mean-energy correction in Section 3 is not a conditional Auger
spectrum.
The ADS API was attempted first for further literature discovery, but name
resolution failed in this environment; subsequent public searches were
restricted to source and publisher records. Those searches did not provide a
final, machine-readable joint cascade for the complete Mg/Fe/Ca target set.

At 100 eV, the rigorous interval for the **sum of all emitted electron
kinetic energies** in a +2 event is `[0, E - sum(IP)]`: Mg I has upper bound
77.318 eV, Mg II 4.821 eV, Fe I 75.898 eV, Fe II 53.149 eV, Fe III
14.436 eV, Ca I 82.015 eV, and Ca II 37.215 eV. These are independent of
the number or distribution of electrons. The lower bound zero allows all
available non-binding energy to remain in fluorescence or final-ion
excitation. The PH1 primary-electron prescription is `K_primary=E-E_PH1`;
its compatibility with the +2 interval requires `E_PH1 >= sum(IP)`.
Ca II 3s violates that inequality by 2.415 eV at *every* photon energy,
not only at its onset.

The charge-only 64-by-64 matrix diagnostics store one **unit shell-absorption
generator** at a time: the source column has `-1`, destination `+1` has
`P_1`, and destination `+2` has `P_2`, with column sum zero. This is a
mapping and conservation check, not a rate matrix to insert into the solver:
each shell needs its own photon-integrated absorption rate. At 100 eV, six
of nine shells permit a KM charge-marginal lookup after both their PH1 and
KM vacancy edges. Mg II 2s and Fe III 3p have KM vacancy edges above 100 eV;
Ca II 3s has the incompatible PH1 primary budget. These three are explicitly
unresolved, and no probability is renormalized or inserted for them. For Fe II
3p, the interval from the PH1 edge 76.17 eV to the higher KM vacancy edge
82.9 eV is likewise unresolved. Even where a lookup is displayed, KM's
vacancy-conditioned marginal is **not** a verified energy-dependent charge
yield for MoCHII's PH1 cross section and is not a heat calculation.

Two energy contradictions are especially direct if one combines an unchanged
KM mean Auger energy with the PH1 primary photoelectron and the selected
final ionization threshold: Mg II 2s gives `95.179 + 20.1 - 98.81 =
16.469 eV`; Ca II 3s gives `62.785 + 7.1 - 60.37 = 9.515 eV`. These positive
excesses occur before fluorescence or residual excitation. They expose an
incompatible combination of source averages and thresholds, not a measured
error in individual physical cascades. Setting a negative residual to zero
or assigning one mean Auger energy to each charge branch would not fix the
physics. Below a final-state or source-vacancy threshold, a forbidden yield
cannot be normalized onto a different charge state without a conditional
calculation.
The bound JSON checks this deliberately invalid mean-energy assignment for
all nine channels: it also produces positive excess for Mg I 2p, Fe I 3p
(one KM subshell), Fe II 3p, and Fe III 3p. Since KM's Auger mean includes
different charge outcomes, these arithmetic excesses are rejection tests for
that assignment, not assertions that the underlying measured events violate
energy conservation.

Cloudy c25.00's `data/mewe_nelectron.dat` and `data/mewe_fluor.dat` reproduce
charge and photon *marginals*, with documented source-specific modifications
in the latter. Its `data/auger_spectrum.dat` contains discrete emitted-electron
energies and average numbers attributed in its header to Dwek and Smith
(1996). The file has entries for elemental C, O, Mg, Si, and Fe, but no Ca and
no separate Mg II, Fe II, or Fe III entries. It is read by
`source/grains.cpp:ReadAugerData` and consumed by grain photoelectric handling;
it is not a gas-ion joint charge/energy cascade table. The gas charge solver
`source/ion_solver.cpp` consumes separate electron-count yields and clips
products at its configured highest stage. Cloudy's gas heat path
`source/cont_gammas.cpp` uses a separate threshold-based energy estimate.
MOCASSIN v2 reads an Auger probability file without using it in its invoked
adjacent metal balance, while v3 adds yield-weighted terms using an existing
population-ratio approximation. None of these invoked paths supplies the
joint energy distribution required here.

The local Jena Atomic Calculator source supports photoionization amplitudes,
stepwise radiative and autoionizing transition rates, and cascade simulation.
This is a plausible *calculation method* for a new state-resolved source, not
a precomputed Mg/Fe/Ca result. Its checked demos do not supply the nine
target vacancy cascades, and the Julia executable is absent in the current
environment. The [JAC cascade documentation](https://openjac.github.io/JenaAtomicCalculator.jl/stable/api-cascades.html)
and [published computational description](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-8994/13/3/520)
describe capabilities; no target-ion calculation or physical validation was
performed here. Targeted searches identified partial Fe II charge-resolved
photoionization measurements and neutral-Mg cascade studies, but no verified
final, machine-readable joint branch set spanning all nine source vacancies.
That search result does not establish that such data do not exist.

## Candidate physical representation, pending an identified calculation

For a physically specified initial ion and shell, a vacancy state `v` would
have a measured or calculated excitation energy `V_v` relative to the source
ion and a state-resolved photoionization cross section. At photon energy `E`,
its primary electron has `K_0 = E - V_v >= 0`. Each permitted Auger edge
`v -> w` has a conditional probability from its partial width and an electron
kinetic energy `V_v - V_w > 0`; each radiative edge has its own width and
photon energy. An endpoint `f` has energy `V_f = sum(IP to final charge) +
E_excitation`. A complete path then satisfies

`E = K_0 + sum(K_Auger) + sum(E_fluorescence) + sum(IP) + E_excitation`.

The probability of a final charge is the sum of **complete path**
probabilities ending in that charge, conditioned on the vacancy state created
at `E`. This construction determines both the charge yield and the energy
distribution from the same transition graph. The event is forbidden below
its *state-specific* threshold; the PH1 cross section, vacancy energies, and
cascade rates must use a coherent threshold convention. KM Table 2 yields
can check marginal charge counts, but cannot determine this graph. At 30 eV
the current secondary-electron code changes behavior, so a mean electron
energy also cannot reproduce the nonlinear deposition of a spectrum spanning
that threshold.

The next acceptable source would need state-resolved photoionization into the
vacancies actually open below 100 eV, radiative and Auger partial widths,
all intermediate and final-state energies, a documented treatment of
unrepresented shake processes, and source uncertainty. Validate its marginal
charge yields against KM or measurements only where thresholds and initial
states match. Verify every path's charge and energy conservation and compare
the resulting full electron spectra with any available primary measurements.
Then assess the effect on the existing 64-stage balance and thermal solution;
no 165-stage expansion is part of this objective. No cascade calculation or
plasma run was started because the required joint source has not been
identified and reviewed.
