# Final Fe I and Ca I numerical-source access check

Date: October 10, 2026. This investigation sought published numerical data for two missing electron-cooling stages. It did not import an atomic table or change production code. The measured URL responses and the locally held final Fe I article hash are in [source_access.json](source_access.json).

## Fe I: Conroy et al. 2020

The final [Conroy et al., *Astrophysical Journal* **902**, 68](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/abb3fd) PDF remains available locally at `/nfs/mocafe/kiseon/MoCHII/references/Conroy_2020_ApJ_902_68.pdf` (SHA-256 `61ad8ac430f9f9f51a7683a65f2da696008b83c66516c65cf8c505918fe93278`). Its Tables 5 and 6 identify complete machine-readable level and effective-collision-strength tables, and the conclusion names them `fe_i_nrg.dat` and `fe_i_coll.dat`. The PDF has zero embedded files. The article's C3 calculation covers 485 retained bound fine-structure states and 19 temperatures from 1,000 to 100,000 K. Its printed excerpts cannot substitute for the complete matrix.

The NASA ADS API confirmed bibcode `2020ApJ...902...68C` and a `DATA` property, but its actual `data` field is only `SIMBAD:1`; `links_data` contains a SIMBAD reference, the article PDF, and the article DOI, with no link to either numerical table. The Crossref article record has no related dataset identifier. Crossref returned HTTP 404 for the four candidate AAS-style data DOI suffixes `/data1` through `/data4`. The official IOP article HTML request returned HTTP 200 only after redirecting to Radware bot-verification HTML; its payload was not the article or numerical data. Earlier official content-server guesses for two MRT names returned 404, while requests for the original `fe_i_*.dat` names returned 403. Guessed names and revisions cannot establish that the files were never published.

The prior [radiative identity audit](../fe_i_radiative_identity/README.md) directly compared the final PDF's first 50 level records with local Stout energy and NIST-dated A files. It found 49 consistent configuration/term/parity/J/energy identities and one discrepancy at level 27. Conroy's own calculation reorders higher target levels. Thus a downloaded collision table would still require a 485-state identity mapping and a coherent radiative model; Stout and Conroy rows cannot be joined by integer index. The present accessible evidence does **not** yield an implementable 1,000–100,000 K Fe I cooling atom.

The exact remaining source request is the final article's complete `fe_i_nrg.dat` and `fe_i_coll.dat`, including table metadata and redistribution terms. No author or publisher was contacted.

## Ca I: Zatsarinny, Parker, and Bartschat 2019

The NASA ADS API confirmed [*Physical Review A* **99**, 012706](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.99.012706), bibcode `2019PhRvA..99a2706Z`. Its ADS links and Crossref registration expose the publisher article and an accepted manuscript, but no deposited numerical cross-section dataset. The official APS supplemental path returned HTTP 401 with HTML, while the APS short supplemental URL redirected to the abstract page as HTML. Neither response is a data table. The accessible [publisher abstract](https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.99.012706) describes cross sections among 39 physical **LS states** from threshold to 100 eV, tested with 444 additional pseudostates. The author-posted typeset final text states that the entire numerical dataset is available electronically **upon request**; no complete numerical matrix was found through the official associated links in this check. No author was contacted.

The local Stout Ca I file has 780 **J-resolved** levels, 136 radiative A records, and no tabulated electron rates, as measured in the earlier [Ca I source audit](../../ca_i_excitation_sources.md). An LS transition cannot be distributed among its fine-structure components without a derived and justified recoupling rule, and the sparse A file does not establish radiative closure. A related [Osorio et al. 2019 *Astronomy & Astrophysics* article](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201834680) is indexed as using an extension of the Zatsarinny Ca I calculation in a stellar model atom. A targeted search found no public state-to-state numerical matrix in the article or archive listings; this is an access observation, not a proof of absence. Its publisher PDF request returned HTTP 403 here, so its final methods have not been verified or adopted as a substitute for the Zatsarinny article. The exact missing material is the BSR-483 state-to-state numerical cross sections with target labels, energies, incident-energy convention, and redistribution terms, plus a compatible radiative model.

The [APS abstract](https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.99.012706), [ADS article record](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019PhRvA..99a2706Z/abstract), [AAS MRT description](https://journals.aas.org/mrt-overview/), and [IOP MRT format description](https://ioppublishing.github.io/content-documentation/Articles/machine_readable_tables_mrt.html) are primary publication and format sources. A registered AAS `/dataN` DOI is a plausible access route for some articles, but its absence for Conroy does not negate the article's explicit machine-table statement.

No new final journal PDF or raw data was saved: the Fe I final PDF was already present, and no additional final PDF or original numerical table was successfully retrieved. No HTML response was misidentified as a PDF or source table.
