# Si I radiation and Mg I electron-excitation source completion audit

## Physical finding

The Si I 14/15 radiative defect has an identified **calculation**, but not a verified final replacement probability. The official NIST MCHF calculation supplies the exact state pair and an electric dipole line strength. The CHIANTI A and weighted oscillator strength are approximately twice the single NIST calculation record, while CHIANTI also adopts a wavelength from the observed energies whose order reverses the calculated energy order. Rescaling the NIST length-form line strength to the observed physical frequency gives `A(15 -> 14) = 5.63129 s^-1`; that is a source-derived **candidate**, not a measured or published accepted rate. The calculation's length and velocity line strengths differ by a factor of 3.388, and the final 2005 journal methods and exact transition table could not be read. Finite-density Si I installation therefore still needs physical acceptance of the chosen calculation and its uncertainty.

For Mg I, the original Barklem et al. BSR/CCC published matrices and the author's separately maintained public directory contain only 500–20,000 K temperatures. No verified published 100,000 K effective-strength data were found. CHIANTI has positive numerical strengths on the tested 1,000–100,000 K grid, including both rows with negative **infinite-temperature** extrapolation endpoints. This supports a finite-domain numerical approximation if explicitly selected, but does not establish a source-backed high-temperature physical accuracy range.

## Si I: exact state and rate provenance

The prior [Si I identity audit](../si_i_radiative_identity/README.md) gives the full level mapping and original CHIANTI hashes. CHIANTI states 14 (`3s2 3p 4p 1P1`, even, `g=3`, observed 47284.062 cm^-1) and 15 (`3s2 3p 3d 1D2`, odd, `g=5`, observed 47351.555 cm^-1) have physical emission `15 -> 14`, with observed gap 67.493 cm^-1. Its `.wgfa` row instead lists `15 14`, wavelength 1,481,635.13 Angstrom, `gf=0.03558`, and `A=132.8 s^-1` as if 15 were the lower state. The wavelength agrees with the magnitude of the observed gap; it cannot settle the emitting identity.

The official [NIST MCHF Si I collection 16](https://nlte.nist.gov/MCHF/Elements/Si/Si_14.16.html) identifies a 2002 *ab initio* MCHF calculation. Its [original `.lsj` data](https://nlte.nist.gov/MCHF/Elements/Si/Si_14.16.cff-mchf.lsj.db) contain one state-resolved `3s(2).3p_2P.3d_1D*` (`g=5`) to `3s(2).3p_2P.4p_1P` (`g=3`) E1 transition: calculated energies 47188.06 and 47317.64 cm^-1, gap 129.58 cm^-1, length-form strength `S=45.1996` atomic units, weighted `GF=0.0177909`, and `A=66.4201 s^-1` for the **calculated** upper `g=3`. The following original line gives velocity-form `S=13.3406`, `GF=0.00525095`, and `A=19.6037 s^-1`. The NIST display table lists two nearly identical records for this state pair; the original `.lsj` has one. CHIANTI's `gf` and A match twice the length-form original within file rounding (`1.99990` and `1.99939`), consistent with duplicate counting but not proof of the unavailable preparation procedure. Another CHIANTI row, 15/26, also has about twice the NIST displayed single transition rate where NIST displays duplicate records; that observation warrants a separate full-source audit before attributing a general importer error.

Holding this single NIST length-form E1 strength fixed while using the observed photon gap and physical emitting weight 5 gives `A = 2.02613e18 S / (g_emitter lambda_A^3) = 5.63129 s^-1`. The corresponding *conditional* velocity-form result is `1.66207 s^-1`; it is not an uncertainty interval. NIST explicitly cautions that its Breit-Pauli velocity operator omits some relativistic terms when term mixing is significant. The large disagreement still prevents treating the length-form candidate as a precision measurement. The Einstein-frequency transformation is physically defined and conserves the photon energy, but the near-degenerate line strength itself has not been independently validated. Neither retaining 132.8 s^-1 after index reversal nor computing A from CHIANTI's doubled `gf` represents the single MCHF calculation.

ADS identified the final [Froese Fischer (2005), *Physical Review A* 71, 042506](https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.71.042506), DOI `10.1103/PhysRevA.71.042506`, bibcode `2005PhRvA..71d2506F`. The APS final PDF request returned HTTP 401. Its accessible abstract concerns calculations through the 3p3d manifold and highlights agreement of length/velocity oscillator strengths for 3p2-to-3p3d allowed lines; it does **not** certify this 3p3d-to-3p4p near-degenerate pair. The exact final-paper methods and rate for that pair remain unverified. The Dufresne et al. (2021) CHIANTI paper's [final publisher methods](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/505/3/3968/6283727) describe additional MCHF/ATSP2K Si I radiation, but do not identify the individual 14/15 source row. The `.wgfa` header mixes several provenance statements, so header attribution alone cannot replace a state-resolved source check.

The current reader keeps the raw row, the actual writer rejects its reversed radiative energy direction, and total ground-state coronal cooling is still mathematically defined because all 35 states can decay to the sole closed ground state. See the prior audit for direct execution evidence. Individual line emission and finite-density populations still depend on a coherent A matrix. No Si I native atom or fit was generated here.

## Mg I: published and transformed temperature coverage

ADS identified the final [Barklem et al. (2017), *Astronomy & Astrophysics* 606, A11](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/abs/2017/10/aa30864-17/aa30864-17.html), DOI `10.1051/0004-6361/201730864`, bibcode `2017A&A...606A..11B`. The archived final Version of Record `~/MoCHII/references/Barklem_etal_2017_AA_606_A11_VoR.pdf` states 25 LS physical states, 300 LS transitions, and effective strengths at 500 K, 1,000–10,000 K in 1,000 K steps, 15,000 K, and 20,000 K. The official [CDS tables](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/ReadMe/J/A%2BA/606/A11?format=html&tex=true) and the author's [public Mg+e directory](https://github.com/barklem/public-data/tree/master/inelastic-Mg%2Be) expose the same 13 temperatures for both BSR and CCC. The public directory was listed directly through GitHub's public contents API: 26 matrix files plus `ReadMe` and `states.dat`, with no higher-temperature matrix or energy-resolved cross-section file. Its [ReadMe](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/barklem/public-data/master/inelastic-Mg+e/ReadMe) identifies symmetric 25-by-25 effective-strength matrices. Its repeated “for 1000 K” descriptions beside several different numbered files are apparent text errors; the filenames, final article, and CDS descriptions establish the listed temperature grid.

The original CHIANTI `mg_1.scups` header says R. P. Dufresne prepared its 15 fine-structure state values on February 27, 2024 by splitting the LS BSR strengths with Autostructure distorted-wave ratios. It warns against spectroscopic diagnostics and says the Burgess–Tully infinite-temperature points were extrapolated from the last three points. The source has 96 bound collision rows. Only rows `(2,12)` and `(13,15)` have a negative ordinate, at the mathematically infinite-temperature endpoint (`-0.009492` and `-0.0007207`). Their last finite nodes correspond to approximately 999,422 and 999,466 K with positive strengths `0.0447` and `0.1719`. At 100,000 K the unchanged CHIANTI reader returns positive `0.1299045841` and `1.3156822425`; all 96 rows were positive at 801 logarithmically spaced temperatures from 1,000 to 100,000 K, with the smallest value `0.0123087169`. These checks establish finite numerical behavior, not source accuracy above 20,000 K. Dropping, clipping, or changing the negative infinite endpoint would modify the represented source and needs a precisely stated finite-domain interpolation rule if adopted.

The existing [direct comparison](../source_validity_ne_i_mg_i/mg_i_bsr_comparison.json) summed all CHIANTI fine-state strengths by LS identity against the published BSR matrices. For the worst pair, LS terms 3 and 4, the relevant fine states are singleton CHIANTI IDs 5 (`3s3p 1P1`) and 6 (`3s4s 3S1`); therefore its discrepancy is not caused by distributing one LS rate among multiple J states. At 500, 1,000, and 20,000 K, respectively, the invoked CHIANTI reader gives `1.43257675`, `1.66203639`, and `1.56397430`, whereas the published BSR entries are `0.657`, `1.24`, and `1.54`. The ratios are `2.18048`, `1.34035`, and `1.01557`. The first CHIANTI source ordinate is itself `1.24` at Burgess–Tully abscissa zero, while its first finite 1,000 K node is approximately `1.662`; the cold discrepancy arises in the supplied conversion/interpolation representation, not from this project's radiation handling. The CHIANTI preparer's original conversion code and higher-temperature scattering results were not available, so its precise cause and the physical error above 20,000 K remain undetermined.

A source-backed restricted Mg I model could use the directly published LS matrices only over 500–20,000 K, after constructing a consistent LS energy/radiation model; direct insertion into the current fine-state atom would mix identities. Alternatively, the existing CHIANTI fine-state data can be named an explicit finite-domain numerical approximation if the physical decision accepts the 1,000 K mismatch and unpublished 20,000–100,000 K continuation. Its two negative infinite-limit nodes must not be serialized as physical effective strengths. A finite-source representation would require a specified domain and verification against the unchanged CHIANTI values, rather than invented positive asymptotes. This audit does not authorize either production choice.
