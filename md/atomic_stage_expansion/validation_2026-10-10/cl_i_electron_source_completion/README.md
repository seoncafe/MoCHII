# Cl I ground fine-structure electron-excitation source

## Physical finding

A calculation of the required **atomic** Cl I electron transition exists: Wang, Zatsarinny, Bartschat, and Booth, *Physical Review A* **87**, 022703 (2013), DOI [10.1103/PhysRevA.87.022703](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.87.022703), ADS bibcode `2013PhRvA..87b2703W`. Its published abstract explicitly identifies electron-induced collisions between neutral chlorine's `3p5 2P^o_{3/2}` ground state and `3p5 2P^o_{1/2}` first excited state. These are the required lower `J=3/2, g=4` and upper `J=1/2, g=2` states, respectively. The local Stout Cl I levels place them at `0` and `882.351 cm^-1`, with spontaneous `A_21=0.0123 s^-1`. The local MAPPINGS constant effective strength `3.120` instead uses weights `g=(2,4)` and has no verified electron-scattering provenance. Its established weight error is documented in [the local diagnostic](../mappings_remaining_excitation/README.md). Neither that constant nor Cloudy's electron-labeled `4.7e-8 cm^3 s^-1` row is an established Cl I **electron** source: the latter traces to a proton approximation in Hollenbach and McKee's Table 8 footnote.

The official [APS accepted manuscript](Wang_2013_APS_accepted_manuscript.pdf) was obtained for source discovery and has SHA-256 `e37cc128093a52ba3c521c85f80593381b9d59fe6bd2c016bc480ba42cb7860b`. Its methods describe a B-spline R-matrix calculation with semirelativistic Breit-Pauli fine structure and polarized pseudostates, and its Figure 2 shows both excitation and deexcitation cross-section curves. It has no numerical cross-section table or supplementary numerical file. Its conclusion says the semirelativistic data are available from the authors on request. **This is an accepted manuscript, not the final published article; its methods and figure details are provisional until checked against the final journal version.** The final APS PDF returned HTTP 401 from the journal PDF endpoint. No authors were contacted.

Kemaneci, Carbone, Booth, Graef, van Dijk, and Kroesen, *Plasma Sources Science and Technology* **23**, 045002 (2014), DOI [10.1088/0963-0252/23/4/045002](https://doi.org/10.1088/0963-0252/23/4/045002), ADS bibcode `2014PSST...23d5002K`, subsequently derived a Maxwellian `e + Cl(2P_{3/2}) -> Cl(2P_{1/2}) + e` rate fit from Wang's cross section. The indexed final article's Table 4 identifies it as reaction 17 and states a fit interval of **electron temperature `T_e=0.5–10 eV`**, equivalent to approximately `5,802–116,045 K`. This intersects but does not cover the full requested `1,000–100,000 K` numerical domain. The [university repository](https://research.tue.nl/en/publications/global-volume-averaged-model-of-inductively-coupled-chlorine-plas/) labels its linked PDF as the final published version; that PDF returned HTTP 403, while the IOP `/pdf` request returned HTML rather than a PDF. Search-indexed text and a later thesis reproduce an analytic reaction-17 rate, but the `T_e^-2` exponent sign differs between those renderings. No coefficient or sign is adopted here without direct final-table inspection. There is no justified continuation below `0.5 eV` from that published fit alone.

## Exact missing inputs and route to use

For a physically identified two-level electron model over `1,000–100,000 K`, obtain the final Wang article and its numerical excitation cross section, including points close enough to the `882.351 cm^-1` threshold for Maxwellian integration at `1,000 K`, or another published rate explicitly valid to that temperature. The accepted manuscript's Figure 2 alone is a plot, and digitizing it would add an unquantified near-threshold interpolation error. Verify the final Kemaneci reaction-17 formula independently if using its higher-temperature fit. The original Wang cross section may be available from the authors as the accepted manuscript says, but no contact is authorized or made in this work.

With a verified upward Maxwellian coefficient `q_12(T)` in `cm^3 s^-1`, the physical effective collision strength is `Upsilon(T)=q_12(T) g_1 sqrt(T) exp[DeltaE/(k_B T)]/(8.629e-6)`, using `g_1=4`; downward detailed balance uses `g_2=2`. The collision source must remain electron-specific. No constant `Upsilon`, proton coefficient, invented cold extrapolation, or level-weight reversal can fill the missing temperatures. The existing Stout radiative `A_21` and observed energy identify the photon transition but do not determine its electron impact rate.
