The diagnostic does not determine whether gf, A, level association, frequency convention, or a combination of heterogeneous calculations caused these differences. A large mismatch is not permission to guess a correction. The original published calculation and conversion must be checked before adopting a source-preserving frequency transformation.

Current dynamic storage can represent the full atom, but successful numerical serialization cannot establish physical source consistency. ## Published cold boundary confirmed

Root obtained and directly read the final Liang, Badnell, and Zhao (2012) article, including Sections 2.1 and 3. Section 3 gives thirteen effective-strength temperatures from 200*(z+1)^2 to 2e6*(z+1)^2 K, with residual charge z=3 for O IV. The published source interval is therefore 3,200--32,000,000 K. The user's already adopted constant-lowest-temperature approximation now holds O IV Upsilon at its 3,200 K value below that source interval; Maxwellian rates and detailed balance retain the actual gas temperature. A direct check passed for all 10,731 collision rows at 1,000, 2,000, and 3,200 K. This is an explicit cold approximation, not a published source accuracy claim below 3,200 K. The shared source reader and serializer temperature metadata were updated; no production product was regenerated. See the [published temperature review](published_temperature_review.json).

An independent original ADF04 retrieval has confirmed the stored A value for the 77/87 example. This establishes that applying the wavelength-based frequency-cubed factor would be unjustified; the original calculation's own energy and transition conventions must govern any transformation. The remaining gf/A inconsistencies are still under direct source investigation.
