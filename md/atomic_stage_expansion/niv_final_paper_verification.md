# N IV final-paper verification

## Source and scope

The inspected source is the final IOP publisher article by L. Fernandez-Menchero,
O. Zatsarinny, and K. Bartschat, *Journal of Physics B* **50** (2017) 065203,
DOI [10.1088/1361-6455/aa5fc4](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6455/aa5fc4).
The local PDF is
`/nfs/mocafe/kiseon/MoCHII/references/Fernandez-Menchero_2017JPhysB_50_065203.pdf`,
SHA-256 `0bcf693a59608c5236082ecb4e5f6e35bd8d6a8e3f98e530851794886cc8de3e`.
Its metadata identifies IOPP as creator, the article DOI, and 13 PDF pages
(12 journal pages). The text, including Sections 2--5 and Tables 1--2, was
read with `pdftotext -layout`. The complete supplemental state and radiative
tables named in the article were not supplied with this PDF and were not
independently inspected.

This verification compares the final paper with the local CHIANTI
`n_4.elvlc`, `.wgfa`, and `.scups` data and Cloudy c25.00 Stout `n_4.nrg`,
`.tp`, and `.coll` files. The direct row and transition-pair audit is recorded
in [the N IV source decision](niv_atomic_source_decision.md); this document
tests its interpretation against the published methods and tables.

## What the paper calculated

Section 2 constructs N IV target wave functions with a B-spline box-based
close-coupling method in Breit--Pauli intermediate coupling. Its expansion
contains term-dependent nonorthogonal orbitals and continuum pseudostates.
The calculation produced approximately 1400 bound and continuum target levels,
from which the authors selected the same **238 target states** used in the
earlier ICFT and DARC comparisons. Section 3 then uses these 238 states in a
Breit--Pauli B-spline R-matrix electron-scattering calculation and computes
effective collision strengths from the collision strengths. Thus the printed
BSR energy, radiative, and collision results arise from one target-state
calculation, whereas the NIST column is an experimental comparison. The paper
does not say that its scattering Hamiltonian used NIST energies. In Section
4.2, the authors explicitly discuss *potential* future adjustment to
recommended energies and note that such an adjustment perturbs the
Hamiltonian and potentially the results.

The complete BSR state table and radiative parameters are said to be in the
online supplement. Table 1 directly gives BSR and NIST energies for the
lowest 40 states. The Stout `n_4.nrg` energies for indices 30--40 reproduce
the paper's BSR column to the shown 0.1 cm^-1 precision; examples are level
30, 494216.6 cm^-1, level 37, 497406.9 cm^-1, and level 40, 502965.3
cm^-1. Across indices 30--238, all 209 Stout energies agree with the CHIANTI
theoretical energies at the latter's integer cm^-1 precision (within 1
cm^-1). This supports, but does not by itself independently prove, identity
with the unavailable complete author supplement for every high state.

## Observed energies and level identity

Stout's header says that its first 20 energies were updated to NIST and the
remainder came from an ADF04 file supplied by the author. Direct row
comparison gives a more precise boundary: Stout **indices 1--29**, including
21--29, reproduce the paper's NIST column at the displayed precision,
whereas indices 30--238 use the BSR column. In particular, at level 29 the
paper prints NIST 487607.4 cm^-1 and BSR 487167.3 cm^-1; Stout uses
487607.4 cm^-1, while the CHIANTI theoretical field is 487167 cm^-1.
Levels 21--29 therefore are an additional observed-energy splice beyond
Stout's stated first-20 update. The selected Stout set is **not** a single
unmodified BSR energy spectrum. This is an important correction to a claim
that its whole energy vector is theoretical and internally generated by the
same wave functions as the rates.

Table 1 calls paper level 29 `2p 3p 3S1` and paper level 37 `2s 4s 3S1`;
their BSR energies are 487167.3 and 497406.9 cm^-1, respectively. Stout
index 29 instead prints a `2s 4s 3S1` label at the observed 487607.4
cm^-1, while index 37 also prints `2s 4s 3S1` at 497406.9 cm^-1. The Stout
index-29 configuration is inconsistent with the paper's assignment.
The current NIST/CHIANTI labeling conflict discussed in the source-decision
record cannot be settled simply by adopting the Stout labels. The strong
index, statistical-weight, A-value, and collision-pair correspondence in
that record is evidence for using the original transition indices, not
evidence that every spectroscopic configuration label is reliable. Do not
swap levels or reverse an Einstein A based on these printed labels.

The article's Table 2 numbers refer to its **full BSR spectrum**, which
contains states outside the selected 238-state expansion. Its levels 88--91
include `2s 6g` states absent from the selected ICFT/CHIANTI set. Hence its
numbering after 87 does not supply a direct index map to the CHIANTI or
Stout 238 rows: paper level 101 (`2p 4s 3P0`, 577512.3 cm^-1) is Stout
level 89, and paper level 272 (`2p 5s 3P0`, 625844.3 cm^-1) is Stout level
137. Matching an index above 87 to a printed Table 2 index without checking
configuration, J, and energy would be incorrect. A direct complete-state
index mapping requires the online supplemental table or author ADF04 file.

## Physical decision and validity

For the existing 136-level CHIANTI rate model, Stout energies are a
**controlled approximate energy choice**, suitable for staging validation.
They preserve positive energy gaps for all 3272 radiative and 9180 collision
pairs, resolve the two exact ties caused by CHIANTI integer rounding, and
retain the demonstrated transition-index mapping. A consistent energy vector
must be used for photon energies, Boltzmann factors, and detailed balance.
The original A values remain attached to their original indexed transitions.

The first-29 NIST splice improves observed low-level thresholds but makes
the energy vector different from the unadjusted BSR scattering Hamiltonian.
It must be identified as an approximation, especially where small resonance
positions dominate low-temperature collision strengths. The source paper
states in Section 4.2 that resonance positions follow target energies, that
low-temperature results may improve after energy adjustment, and that such
adjustment can change the Hamiltonian and higher-temperature results. It
does not validate replacing all observed energies in the CHIANTI file while
leaving its transitions fixed; the observed choices at 107, 134, and 135
cause the verified inverted gaps. It also does not establish that Stout's
particular observed/theoretical splice is a new R-matrix calculation.

The 136-state limit is a **bound-state selection**, not a convergence
criterion: Stout level 136 is 598316.4 cm^-1, below the paper's 624866.0
cm^-1 ionization limit; selected level 137 is 625844.3 cm^-1, above it.
However, Section 2 warns that configuration mixing and omitted nearby states
affect levels above 87. Sections 4 and 5 identify weak and high-state
oscillator strengths and collision rates as uncertain; the authors regard
most *strong, low-lying* transitions through level 87 as substantially more
reliable and cite approximately 20% cross-calculation agreement in the
peak-abundance regime for about 80% of transitions among those levels. That
is an aggregate comparison, not a 20% uncertainty guarantee for every rate,
temperature, or cooling coefficient. Keeping all 136 bound states may be
useful, but the paper cannot establish 136-level cooling convergence.

Production acceptance therefore still requires the targeted numerical
checks specified in the source-decision record: positivity and stationarity
of the rate solve, nonnegative populations and cooling, line contributions
from levels 107, 134, and 135, formerly tied collision pairs, and level-count
convergence over the intended temperature and density range. The final
article resolves the published-method and printed low-state verification
gap; it does not replace these calculations or independently certify every
high-state assignment without the supplemental files.

## Direct checks and limitations

The PDF hash, metadata, article text, and Stout/CHIANTI energy rows were
checked locally. The Stout/CHIANTI comparison used all 238 indices. No
cooling products were regenerated and no rate-matrix or level-convergence
test was run for this source verification; those checks belong to the
production-change validation. The article's complete online supplement was
not inspected, so full 238-state author-index identity remains a qualified
inference from the independent local A/rate mapping, rather than a directly
verified author-table fact.
