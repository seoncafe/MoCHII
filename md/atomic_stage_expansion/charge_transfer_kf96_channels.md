# Product channels and kinetic energy for nine metal charge-transfer boundaries

## Physical finding

For `X^q+ + H(1s) -> X^(q-1)+ + H+`, the kinetic energy released into the gas by a product level `j` is `dE_rec,j = IP[X^(q-1)+] - IP(H) - E_exc,j`. A total rate does not determine its rate-weighted mean. Kingdon and Ferland (1996, *ApJS* **106**, 205–211, bibcode `1996ApJS..106..205K`) Table 1 supplies total-rate fits for all nine reactions below; its Table 2 supplies state-resolved fits for **none** of them. Table 3 concerns selected reverse ionization reactions and does not give these nine product branches. Table 4 gives endothermic reactions, likewise none of these nine. The 1996 paper therefore does not by itself establish `dE_rec` for these reactions.

Kingdon and Ferland (1999, *ApJ Letters* **516**, L107–L109, DOI [10.1086/312008](https://doi.org/10.1086/312008), bibcode `1999ApJ...516L.107K`) **does** publish adopted gas kinetic-energy defects for all nine in its Table 1. Section 2 defines the heat with respect to the continuum, uses a mean defect when multiple product channels contribute, and says that state-resolved results were used where available. Otherwise it estimates the defect from strong avoided crossings at roughly 7–13 bohr, using a representative 9.5-bohr crossing. Its equation and the long-range exit potential imply approximately `27.21(q-1)/9.5 = 2.86(q-1)` eV for the latter estimate. The paper does not label the method or list the product terms and branching fractions separately for each Table 1 entry. Consequently, its Table 1 values support an **adopted, approximate mean kinetic energy**, not a verified state-resolved distribution or a ground-state product.

The repeated 5.73 and 8.60 eV values agree with the 9.5-bohr estimate for `q=3` and `q=4`, respectively. This agreement is evidence of an approximate charge-based prescription, **not** evidence that any identified product term has that defect. The other tabulated values also cannot be assigned a product term by numerical coincidence. KF99 says its avoided-crossing estimates should generally be accurate to much better than 50%; this is the authors' estimate, not an independent uncertainty measurement for the nine reactions. Its treatment assumes ground-state reactants and negligible collisional redistribution of the excited product at nebular densities. Radiative cascades were excluded from its local gas thermal balance; their photons can affect the radiation field and require separate treatment if retained locally.

## Rate-source trace and source access

The KF96 Table 1 references above were matched to the published fit coefficients and row sequence, since PDF text extraction loses some superscript-charge alignment. Ref. 6 is Butler, Heil, and Dalgarno (1980), *ApJ* **241**, 442; ref. 8 is Gargaud, Hanssen, McCarroll, and Valiron (1981), *J. Phys. B* **14**, 2259; ref. 15 is Gargaud, McCarroll, and Opradolce (1989), *A&A* **208**, 251; ref. 16 is Butler and Dalgarno (1980), *ApJ* **241**, 838, [final journal scan](https://adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1980ApJ...241..838B). These are **rate provenance**, not proof that the KF99 heat was obtained from those papers. The Butler and Dalgarno article describes Landau–Zener estimates and identifies emission lines for products it regards as unambiguous; its state-specific branching for the five relevant boundaries has not been extracted or validated here. The remaining cited final journal articles likewise require direct inspection before their state-specific information can be claimed.

The local final KF96 journal scan is `md/heii_h_charge_exchange/references/Kingdon_Ferland_1996_ApJS_106_205.pdf` (SHA-256 `9de90ffa22b42fe59aea23b42c4fd2daa37a5efb0ff89d80355d5e11a227cf26`), read using `pdftotext -layout`, including Section 2 and Tables 1–4. The original final KF99 publisher PDF was obtained from [IOPscience](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1086/312008/pdf) and saved as `md/atomic_stage_expansion/references/Kingdon_Ferland_1999_ApJ_516_L107.pdf` (SHA-256 `2c681fe32532baddee5d19a67e472cefc51f6a0135de01ab82eb9aa182e78def`). Its Section 2 and Tables 1–2 were read with `pdftotext -layout`; the nine Table 1 entries were directly checked against that PDF. The ADS API was attempted first but DNS resolution for `api.adsabs.harvard.edu` failed. Final PDFs for KF96 refs. 6, 8, and 15 were not obtained in this investigation. The exact level assignments and channel fractions therefore remain unverified. The published KF99 values can be cited as adopted thermal defects with the stated approximation and scope.

`~/RT_Codes/Verner_Fortran_sub` contains photoionization, recombination, and collisional-ionization fits, not a KF96 channel or defect table. Both inspected MOCASSIN versions (`~/RT_Codes/MOCASSIN/mocassin-mocassin.2.02.73.2/source/update_mod.f90` and `~/RT_Codes/MOCASSIN/MOCASSIN-3.0/source/update_mod.f90`) transcribe total charge-transfer fit coefficients and selected reverse Boltzmann factors; they do not provide product-level branching or the nine mean recombination defects. Cloudy c25.00 `data/ctrecombdata.dat` contains the same nine numeric heat values, and `source/atmdat_char_tran.cpp` uses its last column as gas heat, but the primary attribution is now the KF99 Table 1, not Cloudy's metadata.

## Implementation consequence

The nine KF96 total-rate fits in `tools/fitting/charge_exchange_expansion.py` and the nine KF99 mean defects now have distinct published sources. For selective staging under the ground-state-reactant, low-density approximation, the existing `CXHEAT` `dE_rec` field can carry each published mean for the recombination direction; the source and approximation must be stated at the row's point of use. This does not require an invented product term. `CXHEATTAB` is for a temperature-dependent ionization defect and does not supply missing recombination branches. Form 9 requires actual product-term energies and forward rates, so it must wait for verified state-specific data and its reverse detailed-balance treatment. No production data or code were changed in this investigation, and no stage was activated.
