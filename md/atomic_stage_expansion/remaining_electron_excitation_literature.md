# Electron-excitation sources for Ar I, Fe I, and Ca III

Date: October 10, 2026. This is a source-access and physical-scope audit, not an adopted cooling model. Counts below come from parsing the actual files named here. A radiative or level file without electron-impact rows cannot supply electron cooling. None of these three ions was added to the production atomic tables in this investigation.

The OPEN-ADAS Ar I raw file has 595 collision rows with 15 numerical entries after the A coefficient, although the header explicitly prints only 14 temperature coordinates. The [official ADF04 type-3 specification](https://www.adas.ac.uk/man/appxa-04.pdf) identifies the trailing entry as the optional Bethe high-energy limit, not a fifteenth temperature node. Its 595 pairs equal the complete 35-choose-2 set. It has 104 strictly positive listed A coefficients; the other 491 A fields are zero. The Fe I file has all 45 pairs among its ten levels and 30 A coefficients greater than 10^-25 s^-1. These counts do **not** show that either source is complete for all physical cooling transitions: the retained level set, radiative decay structure, and ionization boundary must be assessed together.

## Published physics and provenance checks

The Fe I OPEN-ADAS file itself attributes its fine-structure electron strengths to Pelan and Berrington, *Astronomy and Astrophysics Supplement Series* **122**, 177 (1997), DOI [10.1051/aas:1997328](https://doi.org/10.1051/aas:1997328). It says the energies are from NIST and the A values are from a Cowan calculation. The first five levels are the `3d6 4s2` ground-term fine structure; levels 6–10 are `3d7 4s` fine structure. The note calls these the lowest three terms, while the ten listed rows display two configurations with different angular terms; the exact term labeling should be checked against the final article before translating the file. The tabulated temperature interval, not a separately established accuracy interval, supports the present 100–3,980 K numerical use.

The [final Conroy et al. (2020) article](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/1538-4357/abb3fd/pdf), *Astrophysical Journal* **902**, 68, DOI [10.3847/1538-4357/abb3fd](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/abb3fd), was downloaded and its methods and Tables 5–6 inspected. It uses a relativistic DARC electron-scattering calculation with a 485-level retained bound set after shifting to spectroscopic energies. The article reports theoretical-energy discrepancies of about 20% for some low `3d6 4s2` levels and substantial high-level reordering, especially beyond level 150. This matters for a full 485-level level match. The article's own 1,000–100,000 K temperature interval coincides with the present target, but direct table access and level/A matching are prerequisites to implementation. The article does not itself supply a complete machine-readable A table in the material inspected.

The older OPEN-ADAS Ar I file labels its 35 levels as LS terms, but its comment about `2p5nl,2s2p6nl` orbitals does not match the listed Ar `3p5nl` configurations. This appears to be a copied provenance comment; it is an inference, not a confirmed correction. The [official ADAS terminology](https://open.adas.ac.uk/terminology) explains that the apparent `J=4` in the file's `3s2 3p5 4s1 (3)1(4.0)` term is the LS statistical-weight encoding `(g-1)/2=4`, so that term has `g=9`; it is not a fine-structure level with physical J=4. The [Ballance and Griffin (2008) neutral-argon study](https://doi.org/10.1088/0953-4075/41/6/065201) used an LS-coupled R-matrix with pseudostates calculation, as described in the later [A&A Ar III study](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/pdf/2009/24/aa11743-09.pdf). The file's 35 terms and 595 collision rows can be used for an LS-term atom only after its radiative rates, statistical weights, and source conditions are checked. Directly splitting an LS Upsilon among fine-structure levels would introduce an unvalidated approximation. The Auburn deposit instead describes Breit–Pauli treatment and therefore merits priority for level-resolved Ar I when its file is accessible. Its validity range has not been read from the source file.

The final Ballance and Griffin (2008) publisher PDF was requested, but the returned content was HTML rather than a PDF; the erroneous download was removed. The final Pelan and Berrington (1997) article was not obtained either. Detailed claims about those two calculations' published accuracy limits therefore remain unverified. Their OPEN-ADAS file contents and the directly read later Ar III article support the narrower source statements above.

The final journal version of [Bautista, Lind, and Bergemann (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201731232), *Astronomy and Astrophysics* **606**, A127, was identified through ADS. Its abstract says the electron calculation contains the lowest 46 Fe I LS terms, but the publisher PDF returned HTTP 403 in this environment and its complete numerical table has not been inspected. It remains a possible term-resolved alternative, not a verified replacement for Conroy's fine-structure data.

For Ca III, the OPEN-ADAS search index contains different physical datasets with the same leaf filename. The `nist#20` files have no collision rows; the `cophps#ar/dw` files do. The latter identify Autostructure distorted-wave calculations produced by Alessandra Giunta in 2012. Direct parsing found 180 fine-structure levels, 15,639 distinct electron pairs, and a 410,668.5947 cm^-1 ionization boundary. Exactly the first 39 levels are below it, with all 741 bound pairs. Of their 741 numerically positive A fields, 78 are **exactly** `1.00-30`, leaving 663 other radiative entries. An [independent OPEN-ADAS Ne I ADF04 record](https://open.adas.ac.uk/element/detail/adf04/adas%5D%5B10/cop98%5D%5B10_ls%5D%5Bne0.dat) states: “Unphysical A values. Set A=1.0E-30 for following transitions”. This is evidence for a convention in another file, **not** a Ca III source annotation or a universal ADF04 rule. The Ca III `J=0` to `J=0` first transition also cannot undergo an ordinary single-photon decay, but that does not establish the meaning of every one of the 78 fields. The private parser provisionally omits those 78 radiative rows and retains all 741 electron rows. Under this provisional omission, radiative closure is `[1, 2]`; taking every `1e-30 s^-1` entry literally gives closure `[1]` and changes the strict low-density limit. Neither diagnostic closure is accepted as the physical Ca III atom without confirmation of the missing or weak radiative channels.

The source's first excited bound level is about 259,609 cm^-1 (32.2 eV), while the observed NIST/Stout first excited level is about 203,373 cm^-1 (25.2 eV). This roughly 56,236 cm^-1 (7.0 eV) difference changes the Maxwellian threshold factor substantially at 10,000 K; the small cold-end rate predicted from the theoretical energies therefore cannot justify adopting this file without level-resolved physical reconciliation. The source's theoretical energies, A values, and collision strengths form one calculation; no observed energy or A may be substituted merely by matching row indices. Distorted-wave strengths also omit close-coupling resonances, so the printed 900–18,000,000 K grid is a numerical range, not a published accuracy interval. ADS searches for Ca III/Ca2+ electron-impact R-matrix strengths and a review of the six Ca2+ OPEN-ADAS ADF04 listings found no identified close-coupling Ca III dataset; Ca III photoabsorption and Ca II electron scattering are different processes. This is a source-access result, not proof that such a calculation does not exist. Published work on Ca I, Ca II, Ca IV, or Ca-like Fe VII is not Ca III data.

## Local sources, use conditions, and next conversion checks

The [local-source audit](remaining_cooling_source_audit_2026-10-10.md) reports zero electron rows in the Stout Ar I, Fe I, and Ca III files and now incorporates the final Pelan and Berrington (1995) article scan at `/nfs/mocafe/kiseon/MoCHII/references/Pelan_Berrington_1995_AAS_110_209.pdf`. MAPPINGS v5 `lab/data/lines/RESONDAT2.txt` has two Ar I resonance records with single listed strength/weight values, not a level-resolved temperature grid. `INTERDAT2.txt` contains Ar II and Ca IV but not these three stages. No local MAPPINGS Ca III or Fe I collision table was established.

The downloaded OPEN-ADAS raw files include terms permitting personal use but restricting redistribution with modeling code without express written permission. This report records the restriction; it does not assume permission to place their numerical rows into the repository. The source data remain only in `/tmp` for inspection. The final Conroy publisher PDF was saved under `/nfs/mocafe/kiseon/MoCHII/references/Conroy_2020_ApJ_902_68.pdf`; the complete machine-readable collision table has not been obtained. Source access and reproduction terms should be settled before any data import.

The immediate private numerical candidates are a 39-level Ca III electron model on its 900–18,000,000 K grid, with incomplete radiative-rate evidence, and Fe I's ten-level atom **within its 100–3,980 K table interval**. For a 1,000–100,000 K production calculation, prioritize retrieving Conroy's full published machine-readable table and inspecting the Auburn Ar I ADF04, then verify counts, unique pairs, positive finite Upsilon, A values, ordering, and the bound/ionization boundary. Ca III additionally requires a justified level-by-level energy and radiation reconciliation, source-use permission, and an accuracy assessment of the distorted-wave treatment near threshold. Compare complete cooling-versus-temperature and density profiles against each physically accepted full source before selecting a reduced atom; matching table nodes alone does not establish low-temperature accuracy.

## Additional main-session access checks

The main session read the final Conroy article directly with `pdftotext -layout`.
Tables 5 and 6 explicitly identify the complete machine-readable materials;
the conclusion names `fe_i_nrg.dat` and `fe_i_coll.dat`. No missing collision
strength was inferred from the printed excerpts. The following legitimate
access routes were checked on October 10, 2026:

- IOP official content server, volume 902, issue 1, article 68, `revision1/`
  and `revision2/apjabb3fdt5_mrt.txt` and `apjabb3fdt6_mrt.txt`: HTTP 404.
- The same official content server at `revision1/fe_i_nrg.dat` and
  `revision1/fe_i_coll.dat`: HTTP 403.
- CDS deposited-catalog route `J/ApJ/902/68/ReadMe`: HTTP 404. Candidate
  `table5.dat` and `table6.dat` responses were HTML browser-verification
  pages, not numerical data; these were not parsed or placed in references.
- The authors' [Queen's University Belfast publication record](https://pure.qub.ac.uk/en/publications/electron-impact-excitation-of-fei/)
  exposes the DOI but no complete collision-table download.

These access checks leave the full table contents and compatibility with a
complete radiative atom unverified. They do not change the final article's
verified calculation description or authorize a sparse excerpt as a complete
Fe I cooling source. Full-range Fe I conversion requires the two original
machine-readable files and a coherent radiative-rate source.
