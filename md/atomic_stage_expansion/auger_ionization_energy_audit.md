# Inner-shell Auger ionization and energy audit (100 eV target)

**October 11 source review.** This report's measured reachability counts use
the historical 64-stage roster; the current target is 77 metal stages.
The [mean-energy review](validation_2026-10-11/auger_mean_energy/README.md)
corrects an overly restrictive thermal requirement in the earlier design.
A consistent unconditional mean suffices for full thermalization of metal
electrons. An electron spectrum is needed for nonlinear secondary deposition,
which the current metal path does not compute. The published Eq. (7) gives
a negative Ca II M1 corrected mean; threshold and energy-model reconciliation
remain necessary. No production Auger rate or heating term was activated.

**Physical judgment.** The proposed 64-stage roster contains the products of the nine positive nonadjacent channels identified below, but the current ion balance and gas-energy calculation are physically incomplete when these shells absorb. The existing `species_cross_section` sums shell absorption yet scores every event into the adjacent stage and deposits only the primary photoelectron energy. Activation at 100 eV requires shell-resolved rates, nonadjacent balance, and a traceable electron/photon energy ledger. An effect-size argument cannot remove this requirement.

## Scope and direct measurement

`python3 tools/fitting/audit_auger_channels.py` reads the original Verner `phfit2.f` PH1 tables through `make_element_data.py`, the NIST ionization potentials already used by the element generator, and Cloudy c25.00 `mewe_nelectron.dat`. It evaluates each occupied shell of the eleven-element proposed roster at a photon upper bound of 100 eV. A PH1 shell is counted only when the stage potential, PH1 edge, and PH2-to-PH1 switch energy are all below the band top; a yield channel must also have positive probability and a sum of sequential physical ionization potentials below the band top. Its channel onset is the maximum of those energies. This is **energetic reachability**, not a spectrum-weighted rate or a claim that the plasma populates the source ion.

The direct run found 31 active PH1 shell entries and nine positive, energetically possible inner-shell transitions with two ejected electrons. All nine products fit the proposed roster; none requires a third ejected electron inside 100 eV. The figure [auger_channels.pdf](validation_2026-10-10/auger_audit/auger_channels.pdf) and complete [auger_channels.json](validation_2026-10-10/auger_audit/auger_channels.json) are generated by the script. `current_stage` in the JSON distinguishes present from proposed source ions.

For Ca II 3s, the 62.785 eV product threshold exceeds the tabulated 60.37 eV shell edge; therefore a two-electron event cannot start at the shell edge. An implementation must apply the product threshold and must not blindly use an energy-independent yield below it. The same principle applies to every channel and every photon energy. The table's yields are the **Cloudy transcription**, not independently measured probabilities. Cloudy's shell index differs from the Kaastra and Mewe convention: Cloudy 2s and 3s correspond to CDS L1 and M1, while Cloudy 2p and 3p combine CDS L2/L3 and M2/M3. The CDS Table 2 **does** have Mg I 2s (Z=12, stage=1, shell=2) and Ca I 3s (Z=20, stage=1, shell=5) rows; both give two-electron probability 1.0000. A corrected read-only audit compares all nine possible two-electron channels with their CDS rows in [auger_audit_cds_checked](validation_2026-10-10/auger_audit_cds_checked/auger_channels.json): all nine mappings are complete, with Cloudy and CDS probabilities differing by at most 0.00001. The original [auger_channels.json](validation_2026-10-10/auger_audit/auger_channels.json) remains preserved. The PH1 and Kaastra/Mewe threshold energies differ, so their rates and energy allocations must not be combined without a consistent threshold convention. Cloudy's three Fe 3p rows at stages I–III here are nearly pure two-electron yields; that does not establish exact branch-resolved electron spectra.

## Source and implementation provenance

The local ADS scan `~/RT_Codes/ExoAtmosphere/references/Kaastra_1993_AAS_97_443.pdf` identifies the final article as Kaastra and Mewe (1993), *Astronomy and Astrophysics Supplement Series* **97**, 443–482, bibcode `1993A&AS...97..443K` (SHA-256 `4b22f56f4c38a5f5dc24cd553eb49b17a7b40c87364e9754a635577e79e02cc5`). It is an image scan, so `pdftotext -layout` does not recover its methods; OCR of journal pages 446–447 was read to check the method. Section 2.2 describes a branching cascade, checks the energy of each Auger transition, and drops energetically forbidden branches. Section 3 and Eqs. (5)–(7) compare average Auger electron energy, radiative energy, and sequential final-ion binding energy; the article reports deviations usually within 10% but up to 70% for some M1 vacancies. It attributes part of the discrepancy to final-state excitation and subshell correlation, and introduces a tabulated correction parameter. This makes a direct import of mean `EA` into an exact energy ledger physically unjustified. The local CDS `ReadMe` and deposited `table2` explicitly define pre-ionization stage, vacancy shell, mean Auger electron energy, and probabilities for 1–10 emitted electrons, with probabilities tabulated in units of 1/10000. The deposited `table3` defines fluorescence energy and mean photons emitted for a vacancy; a fluorescence yield can exceed one because a cascade can emit multiple photons. These source-table definitions and the relevant journal methods were checked directly. The remaining pages were not OCR-audited. The ADS API was attempted first and failed DNS resolution in this environment. The local ADS final-journal scan and CDS tables supply the published data; publisher-site access could not be tested here.

Verner's original `~/RT_Codes/Verner_Fortran_sub/phfit2.f:1-23,35-59` defines physical shells 1s through 4s, the first-inner-shell switch, and shell fits. `tools/fitting/make_element_data.py:715-761,905-916` parses those fits and writes `PHSHELL`; `src/species_mod.f90:85-96,1424-1469` stores shell rows and sums their cross sections above `einn`. Crucially, `PHSHELL` stores orbital angular momentum and coefficients, not the original physical shell index; Ca can skip 3d while retaining 4s, so the row ordinal is not a universally safe yield-table key.

Cloudy c25.00 reads `mewe_nelectron.dat` at `source/atmdat_readin.cpp:913-965`; its row `nshell` gives the maximum tabulated number of ejected electrons, including the primary. Its invoked ionization matrix uses each shell's photo rate times the corresponding electron-count probability at `source/ion_solver.cpp:649-668`. That solver clips a product to `IonHigh`; MoCHII should instead reject an unrepresented energetically possible product. Cloudy's gas heating uses its own threshold and secondary-energy approximations (`source/cont_gammas.cpp:382-435`), which are not sufficient evidence for an exact MoCHII energy budget. MAPPINGS 5.2.1 has an invoked `auphot` branch at `src/phion.f:141-145` and a +2 matrix entry at `src/ionab.f:165-180`; this is a useful matrix precedent, but does not provide the full electron-count distribution. MOCASSIN v2 reads yields (`source/hydro_mod.f90:36-54`, invoked from `ph_mod.f90:278`) without an Auger term in its invoked balance; MOCASSIN 3.0 does add yield-weighted terms in `source/update_mod.f90:1833-1855`, with a population-ratio approximation. These distinctions matter: a table reader, a linked routine, and an invoked physical rate are separate facts.

MoCHII's current executable links `species_mod.o`, `photo_xsec.o`, and `ion_stage_equilibrium_mod.o` through `Makefile:98,112,114`. The packet path calls `species_rate_path_score` from `ion_score_mod.f90:220-226`. `species_rate_path_score` keeps only total rate and primary-electron heating for each adjacent transition (`species_mod.f90:491-506`); `species_gamma_compute` converts those totals to `egam/eheat` (`:998-1017`). Both direct and cached abundance paths call the adjacent solver (`:1090-1107,1475-1497`; `ion_stage_equilibrium_mod.f90:1-12`), and `metal_heating` consumes the deficient `eheat` (`species_mod.f90:1266-1283`). The secondary-electron estimator in `ion_score_mod.f90:180-196` loops over H I, He I, and He II only. `gas_rates_mod.f90:126-155` then partitions only those three primary-electron arrays. The defect therefore affects metal charge, metal electron density, line and free-free cooling through their stage fractions, and metal thermal heating.

## MOCASSIN v2 physical-scope boundary

The v2 tree has an invoked shell-resolved opacity calculation: `source/ionization_mod.f90:409-438` loops through metal shells and adds each tabulated shell cross section to continuum opacity. `source/ph_mod.f90:506-580` contains the Verner partial-shell formula. Its cell update is invoked by `source/iteration_mod.f90:808`; `source/update_mod.f90:180-268` nevertheless takes the **outer-shell pointer** `elementP(elem,ion,outShell,3)` for the metal photoionization rate. Its thermal path repeats that choice at `:1127-1173`, then weights the photoheating integral by photon energy minus the outer threshold at `:1200-1229`. The invoked `ionBalance` at `:365,1321-1623` uses an adjacent-stage rate ratio (`:1487-1499`) and product chain (`:1505-1543`); no yield-weighted, nonadjacent transition is present there. `ph_mod.f90:278` calls `makeAugerData`, which reads yield arrays in `hydro_mod.f90:36-54`, but those arrays are not evidence that the invoked v2 abundance or thermal calculation uses them. MOCASSIN 3.0's yield terms (`source/update_mod.f90:1833-1855`) belong to a different code version and must not be attributed to v2.

The calculations have different active stages, spectral inputs, transfer details, and atomic data; no matched model or full-profile comparison was performed.

## Required physical design

1. Preserve the physical shell index when creating atomic data. A `PHSHELL` row needs an explicit index (1s=1 through 4s=7), or a separate verified table keyed by element, source ion, and physical shell index. This changes the atomic input layout and needs the main session's prior confirmation under the repository instructions. The generator must compare each imported yield vector with the deposited source mapping and report any row without a direct CDS counterpart. For the 100 eV target, retain all nine identified channels and any one-electron fluorescence branch of their same shells.
2. At packet energy `E`, obtain each active shell's `sigma_s(E)` and a probability `P_s,m(E)` for total emitted electrons `m`. Require `P_s,m >= 0`, sum over all allowed products to one within source precision, and enforce `E >= sum_{j=i}^{i+m-1} IP_j` before emitting an `i -> i+m` event. Do not renormalize an energetically forbidden empirical branch without a physically justified conditional model. The current PH2 outer fit has no shell partition below `einn`, so its heating/rate should remain a distinct outer-channel approximation with a stated validity range.
3. Score `Gamma_i,m = integral [F_E/E] sum_s sigma_s(E) P_s,m(E) dE` per source stage and destination. Preserve total absorption `sum_m Gamma_i,m = Gamma_i` and build an element-wise continuous-time generator with off-diagonal `Q_{j,i}=Gamma_{i,j}` and `Q_{i,i}=-sum_{j != i}Q_{j,i}`. Add existing adjacent CI, recombination, and charge exchange to the same generator. Solve `Q f=0` with `sum f=1`, nonnegative fractions, and a rank/closed-class check for nonunique equilibria. The existing logarithmic adjacent solver is an exact limiting reference when all nonadjacent rates vanish; all consumers must share the new fraction calculation, including `species_metal_sums` and the opacity cache.
4. For each branch, account for absorbed photon energy by `E = sum IP + K_primary + sum K_Auger + E_fluorescence + E_remaining_excitation`. Partition each electron's kinetic energy into thermal heating, H/He secondary ionization, and secondary excitation with the existing degradation prescription, then count fluorescence or subsequent line photons as radiative output (or transport them if enabled). Current PH1 shell-edge heating `E - E_shell` is only the primary electron term. The deposited Kaastra/Mewe Table 2 mean Auger energy `EA` and Table 3 fluorescence information are possible starting data, but their Lotz edges, shell conventions, and mean/branch conditioning must be reconciled with PH1 and NIST potentials before use. A negative residual or a sum above `E` is a hard error; filling the residual with gas heat is not a physical derivation.
5. Validate shell cross sections against the original `phfit2`, yield normalization and shell mapping against the original deposited tables, and the matrix against the adjacent limit plus finite +2 examples. Check zero and nonzero rates, normalization, charge conservation, terminal destination, and the packet-energy ledger at each shell onset and at 100 eV. A 100 eV source calculation then needs matched input conditions and full temperature, velocity, density, and composition profiles before comparison with another code.
