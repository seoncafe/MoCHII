\begin{abstract}
MoCHII evaluates all metal-line atomic data from analytic fits; the raw
CHIANTI database is never parsed at runtime.  The Python fitting scripts under
\code{tools/fitting/} are the auditable source of every coefficient in
\code{data/atomic/}.  This memo documents the fitting procedure and its two
products: the cooling-function fits that carry the thermal-loop line
cooling in the $n_e \to 0$ limit, 28 ions in the historical CHIANTI fit set (H\,\textsc{i} and
27 metal ions) --- suppressed to the local electron density at run time by a
$(T, n_e)$ table built from the $n$-level solve --- and the $n$-level
data files (level energies, $A$-values, and Chebyshev fits of the
effective collision strengths in Burgess--Tully scaled space) that supply
the output-time line emissivities.  The historical CHIANTI fit set was checked against the
CHIANTI v11.0.2 source data and against PyNeb, and the cooling fits are
additionally checked channel by channel against an independent
photoionization code on a fixed gas state
(\code{docs/MoCHII\_cooling\_analysis.pdf}).
The fitted files reproduce direct CHIANTI evaluation to $\lesssim 0.5\%$ in
the classic diagnostic ratios; the remaining differences from PyNeb
(up to $6$--$10\%$ for the density diagnostics near their critical
densities) trace to PyNeb's independent choice of collision strengths and
$A$-values, not to fit error.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Current selection and scope (2026-10-11).}
The production registry has 43 metal stages and 32 transitions; the
77-stage, 66-transition expansion is staged but not installed.  The default
\code{metal\_excitation\_model = 'mocassin\_v2'} reads metal cooling and
$n$-level files from \code{data/atomic/excitation/mocassin\_v2/}; the
\code{'extended'} selection reads \code{data/atomic/excitation/extended/}.
The selections also determine emitted lines and charge-transfer product
populations.  Hydrogen excitation remains under \code{data/atomic/}.
The two selections currently contain 55 atoms/571 levels/49 fits/763 terms
and 62 atoms/1951 levels/38 fits/377 terms, respectively.  These file counts do not establish atomic accuracy.
Ca\,\textsc{vii} remains unaccepted: 27 levels, 310 rates, and four

\section{Overview: two products, one design rule}
The metal-line design requires that adding an ion be a data operation,
not a code operation.  The fitting scripts therefore produce, for each
registered ion, self-contained coefficient files with provenance headers
(CHIANTI version, population model, fit range, fit form, maximum fit
error), of two kinds:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Cooling fits (thermal balance).}  A smooth closed-form
  cooling function $\Lam_{\rm ion}(T)$, cheap and smooth inside
  the thermal-balance bisection on $\log T$, in the $n_e \to 0$ limit of statistical
  equilibrium.  This is the low-density \emph{carrier}: at run time the
  thermal loop suppresses it to the local electron density with a
  $(T, n_e)$ table built from the same $n$-level solve
  (\code{par\%cooling\_model = local\_ne}, the default), so the thermal
  balance and the diagnostic line list are one physics.
\item \textbf{$n$-level data (diagnostics).}  Level energies,
  statistical weights, radiative $A$-values, and fitted effective
  collision strengths $\Upsilon(T)$ feeding the generic $n$-level solver
  (\code{nlevel\_mod}), evaluated on the converged state for the line
  output and, at setup, to build the $(T, n_e)$ suppression table of the
  thermal balance (\code{nlevel\_cooling\_mod}).  These supply the
  density-dependent emissivities and the diagnostic line ratios that the
  cooling fits cannot give.
\end{itemize}

The fitting scripts are seeded from EXHALE: \code{chianti\_cooling.py} (copied
from \code{EXHALE/cooling\_data/}) reads the raw CHIANTI ASCII ion files
(\code{.elvlc}, \code{.wgfa}, \code{.scups}) directly, with no ChiantiPy
dependency, and applies the Burgess--Tully (1992) descaling of the stored
scaled collision strengths.  CHIANTI v11.0.2 lives at
\code{\char`~/RT\_Codes/CHIANTI/dbase}.

\section{The cooling function}
For a transition $l\to u$ with energy gap $\Delta E_{lu}$, the Maxwellian
electron-impact excitation rate coefficient is
\begin{equation}
  q_{lu}(T) \;=\; \frac{8.629\times 10^{-6}}{g_l\,\sqrt{T}}\,
  \Upsilon_{lu}(T)\, e^{-\Delta E_{lu}/kT}
  \qquad [\mathrm{cm^{3}\,s^{-1}}],
  \label{eq:qlu}
\end{equation}
with $\Upsilon_{lu}(T)$ the effective collision strength obtained by
descaling the \code{.scups} data.  In the optically thin, low-density
limit every collisional excitation is followed by a radiative decay, so
the line cooling normalized by $n_e\,n_{\rm ion}$ is
\begin{equation}
  \Lam_{\rm ion}(T) \;=\; \sum_{l\to u} f_l(T)\, q_{lu}(T)\, \Delta E_{lu}
  \qquad [\ergcm],
  \label{eq:lambda}
\end{equation}
where $f_l(T)$ is the fractional population of the lower level.  The
fitting procedure evaluates the \emph{$n_e \to 0$ limit of statistical
equilibrium}: at $n_e \ll n_{\rm crit}$ of the ground-term fine
structure the metastable levels are drained radiatively faster than
collisions populate them, so every ion sits in its lowest level
($f_1 = 1$) and excitation happens out of that level only.  Each
excitation is followed by a radiative cascade that ultimately radiates
the full $\Delta E_{1u}$ whatever route it takes down, so
$\Lam_{\rm ion}$ is the excitation power out of the ground level and is
exactly what the $n$-level solve of Section~\ref{sec:nlevel} returns as $n_e \to 0$.
$\Delta E_{lu}$ is the observed \code{.elvlc} level difference in both
the Boltzmann factor of Eq.~(\ref{eq:qlu}) and the radiated energy of
Eq.~(\ref{eq:lambda}); the \code{.scups} \code{de} is the
Burgess--Tully \emph{scaling} energy and enters only the scaled
temperature.  Detailed balance ties the excitation rate to the same
level gap that appears in the equilibrium matrix, so the two energies
must not be interchanged.

\section{Cooling-function fits}
Each $\Lam_{\rm ion}(T)$ curve is fitted over $10^3\mbox{--}10^5$~K with
the multi-exponential form used throughout EXHALE,
\begin{equation}
  \Lam_{\rm ion}(T) \;=\; T^{-1/2} \sum_{i=1}^{N} A_i\, e^{-T_i/T},
  \label{eq:cool}
\end{equation}
a Levenberg--Marquardt fit on $\log\Lam$.  In the $n_e \to 0$ limit
Eq.~(\ref{eq:cool}) is the exact form of Eq.~(\ref{eq:lambda}) apart
from the slow temperature dependence of $\Upsilon$: the $T_i$ are the
excitation temperatures $\Delta E_{1u}/k$ and the $A_i$ their
excitation-weighted strengths.  The starting ladder is therefore built
from the level energies themselves --- the transitions out of the ground
level are clustered in $\log(\Delta E_{1u}/k)$, weighted by the share of
the cooling each carries across the fit range --- and the fitter sweeps
$N = 2\ldots8$ with six perturbed restarts at each $N$, stopping at the
first $N$ that holds the maximum error below 1\%; where no $N$ reaches
it, the best fit found over all $N$ is kept (\code{fit\_cooling.py}).
In this historical set, nine of the 28 ions end above 1\%.  Ions with only a few excitation
blocks (the Cl ions have four transitions out of the ground term) are
the hardest case, because a single exponential has a fixed amplitude and
the temperature dependence of $\Upsilon$ has to be carried by
superposing neighboring terms; the three Cl ions end at 7--9\%.  Table~\ref{tab:cool} lists the fit
quality; Figure~\ref{fig:cool} overlays the fits on the CHIANTI curves.
\code{fit\_cooling.py} writes \code{cooling\_<el>\_<stage>.txt} to its
requested output directory (historically \code{data/atomic/}).  Approved
model installation places the matching runtime fits under
\code{data/atomic/excitation/<model>/}.

A caveat carried in every file header: Eq.~(\ref{eq:cool}) is
low-density-limit cooling.  Transitions with low critical density (the
fine-structure IR lines, most sharply [C\,\textsc{ii}] $158\,\mu$m with
$n_{\rm crit} \sim 50$~cm$^{-3}$ and the [O\,\textsc{iii}]
$52,88\,\mu$m pair; [O\,\textsc{ii}] $\lambda\lambda 3726,3729$;
[S\,\textsc{ii}] $\lambda\lambda 6717,6731$) saturate above
$n_{\rm crit}$, where the fit overestimates the cooling.  The thermal
loop therefore treats Eq.~(\ref{eq:cool}) as the $n_e \to 0$ carrier and
suppresses it to the local density: on the Cloudy c25.00 state of the
HII40 benchmark ($n_e \simeq 110$~cm$^{-3}$; the fixed-state comparison of
\code{MoCHII\_cooling\_analysis.pdf}, measured 2026-07-25) the fitted line cooling
exceeds the Cloudy inventory by 11.6\%, of which $+5.1$ points come from
[O\,\textsc{iii}] and $+3.9$ from [C\,\textsc{ii}], while the same ions
evaluated with the $n$-level solve at the local $n_e$ land within 2.4\%
and 6\% and bring the summed line cooling to $1.01$ times Cloudy's.
MoCHII therefore builds a $(T, n_e)$ suppression table from the same
$n$-level solve at setup and applies it in the thermal loop by default
(\code{par\%cooling\_model = local\_ne}); \code{low\_density} uses the
fits directly.  The emissivities themselves always come from the
$n$-level data.

\section{$n$-level data with fitted collision strengths}
\label{sec:nlevel}
For each ion the lowest $n$ levels are exported --- enough for the
optical/IR diagnostics (Table~\ref{tab:nlev}); extra levels are harmless
to the solver.  The file \code{data/atomic/nlevel\_<el>\_<stage>.txt} contains
three blocks:
\begin{itemize}
\item \code{NLEV}: level index, statistical weight $g$, energy in
  cm$^{-1}$ (observed values preferred, theoretical as fallback);
\item \code{NRAD}: radiative rates $A_{ul}$ from \code{.wgfa} (duplicate
  rows summed);
\item \code{NUPS}: one line for each collisional transition --- the
  Burgess--Tully type, scaling parameter $C$, transition energy in Ryd,
  the fitted domain $[s_{\rm lo}, s_{\rm hi}]$, a log flag, and the
  Chebyshev coefficients, followed by the recorded maximum fit error.
\end{itemize}
For C\,\textsc{i}, C\,\textsc{ii} and O\,\textsc{i} a separate file
\code{data/atomic/excitation/<model>/}\\\code{hcoll\_<el>\_<stage>.txt}
(\code{make\_hcoll\_fine\_structure.py}) adds atomic-hydrogen impact rates
on the ground-term fine structure; \code{nlevel\_load} reads it when it
exists.  Atomic-hydrogen impact is included only when
\code{par\%neutral\_hydrogen\_excitation} is enabled.  The historical
expanded iron files and \code{fe\_levels\_full} input selector have been
removed; the selected excitation model now determines the iron atom.

\subsection{Burgess--Tully scaling and Chebyshev fits}
CHIANTI stores $\Upsilon$ on the Burgess--Tully (1992) scaled temperature
$s\in[0,1]$.  With $e_t \equiv kT/\Delta E$, the forward map is
\begin{equation}
  s \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    1 - \dfrac{\ln C}{\ln(e_t + C)}, & \text{types 1, 4},\\[8pt]
    \dfrac{e_t}{e_t + C}, & \text{types 2, 3, 5, 6},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
and the descaling back to $\Upsilon$ is
\begin{equation}
  \Upsilon \;=\; y\ln(e_t+e)~[1];\quad y~[2];\quad
  \frac{y}{e_t+1}~[3];\quad y\ln(e_t+C)~[4];\quad
  \frac{y}{e_t}~[5];\quad 10^{\,y}~[6],
\end{equation}
where $y(s)$ is the scaled value.  Instead of the spline-through-table
evaluation used by CHIANTI itself, the fitting scripts fit $y(s)$ with a
Chebyshev series over the image of $T\in[10^3,10^5]$~K intersected with
the tabulated range,
\begin{equation}
  u = \frac{2(s - s_{\rm lo})}{s_{\rm hi}-s_{\rm lo}} - 1,\qquad
  y = \sum_{k=0}^{n_c-1} c_k\, T_k(u),
\end{equation}
clipping $s$ into $[s_{\rm lo}, s_{\rm hi}]$ outside the fit domain.  The
degree grows from 2 until the descaled $\Upsilon(T)$ matches the direct
spline evaluation to $0.2\%$ (cap at 12).  Weak intercombination
transitions whose tables span decades (e.g.\ O\,\textsc{iii}
$^1\!D_2,^1\!S_0 \rightarrow {}^5\!S_2$) defeat a linear-space fit; for
those the series stores $\log_{10} y$ instead, marked by the log flag
(\code{logf=1}, apply $10^{y}$ before descaling).  The Fortran
\code{nlevel\_mod} evaluates exactly this recipe (Clenshaw summation of
the series); \code{verify\_nlevel\_pyneb.py} replicates it in Python.

\subsection{Statistical equilibrium and emissivities}
Given $(T_e, n_e)$, the solver builds the rate matrix from the file
contents alone (plus the atomic-hydrogen rates where an \code{hcoll}
file exists, entering as $n_{\rm HI} q^{\rm H}$ beside $n_e q$):
collisional rates from Eq.~(\ref{eq:qlu}) with
de-excitation by detailed balance,
$q_{ul} = 8.629\times10^{-6}\,\Upsilon/(g_u\sqrt{T})$ and
$q_{lu} = (g_u/g_l)\,q_{ul}\,e^{-\Delta E/kT}$, plus the radiative
$A_{ul}$; the level populations follow from $\dot{n}=0$ with the closure
$\sum_i n_i = 1$, and the emissivity of a line is
$j_{ul} \propto n_u A_{ul}\,\Delta E_{ul}$.

\section{Adding an ion}
The procedure is a data operation:
\begin{enumerate}
\item add one entry to the \code{IONS} dictionary of \code{fit\_cooling.py}
  (element, stage; the starting ladder comes from the level energies) and
  of \code{fit\_nlevel.py} (element, stage, number of levels);
\item run both scripts; check the reported maximum fit errors and the
  overlay figure;
\item extend \code{verify\_nlevel\_pyneb.py} with the diagnostic ratios
  of the new ion where PyNeb has data, and record the comparison.
\end{enumerate}
A further stage of a registered element, within the stages its
\code{element\_<el>.txt} carries, needs nothing more: at setup
\code{species\_mod} loads \code{cooling\_<el>\_<stage>.txt} for every
such stage where it exists, and \code{lines\_mod} solves every stage that has
an \code{nlevel} file.  A new element is not only data: it also needs its
\code{data/atomic/element\_<el>.txt} (\code{make\_element\_data.py}), an
\code{abund\_<El>} parameter in \code{define.f90}, and an entry in the
element list of \code{species\_mod}.  C, Ar and Fe, which this memo
named as the next additions, have been added, and so have Mg, Si, Cl and
Ca; the registry is the eleven elements C, N, O, Ne, S, Ar, Mg, Fe, Si, Cl,
Ca.
